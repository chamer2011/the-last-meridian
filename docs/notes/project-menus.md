# project-menus

*The menus tidied on 2026-11-02 at the owner's request: seventeen entries grouped under five headings on the rail, long sections folded behind [ + ] lines, three entries renamed, and no explaining text under the names, which they asked for*

On 2026-11-02 the owner said the menus felt cluttered and asked for a couple of options to make them easier to read and to know what's in them. I showed three (five grouped doors with overview pages; the same seventeen entries grouped and labelled with folding sections; a front page of cards) first as chat mock-ups, then, when they asked to keep the console command style, as pictures drawn with the game's own stylesheet. They chose the second, "Option B but without the explaining under the name of the window".

What went in: `MENU_GROUPS` in `public/menus.js` (You: character, skills, traits, perks, beliefs, your past; The story: quests, leads, people, what you know, enemies met; The fleet: fleet, ship, star map; Gear: inventory, market; Game: settings), drawn by `menuButtons` in `public/app.js` as `-- YOU --` headings on the rail and inline on the narrow window's tabs; `fold`/`folded` in `public/dom.js` (a `[ + Title ]` / `[ - Title ]` line, remembered while the game's open); folded by default: Quests' Finished and Journal, Leads' Finished and Moved on, Traits' Everything affecting you, Your past's Life history and Loose ends, Settings' Display, Skill checks, Fights, AI model, What the AI has cost, API key and Your files, Fleet's Rations and Work crews (with the current choice on the line), Officers and council and Fleet log, the Star map's News. Renamed: Dossier to Your past, Codex to What you know, Bestiary to Enemies met (the commands `/dossier`, `/codex`, `/bestiary` and the ids stay). The Leads page's intro paragraph and most of the Star map's went; what each menu holds is a tooltip on its button (`about` in `MENUS`), not on the screen. Then they said the menus felt stiff: opening a part "derailed the whole menu instead of just sliding and collapsing". Cause: the fold redrew the whole drawer, and the pages' CSS multi-column layout rebalanced, moving parts between columns. Fix: a fold now slides open and shut in place (`.fold-body` with `grid-template-rows` 0fr to 1fr over .24s, `inert` and hidden when shut, no redraw), and `columns()` in `public/dom.js` puts a page's parts in two steady columns (`.menu-col`, first half left, rest right, stacking when narrow) so a part that opens moves only what's under it; lists of cards (People, What you know, Enemies met) keep packing into columns as `.menu-cards`. Three more explaining lines went: People's sentence, the Codex's "Underlined words..." and Enemies met's paragraph. Checked in the browser at 1384 and 814 wide with a throwaway script (60 checks); not played by them yet.

**Why:** they judge by playing and found seventeen flat entries and long pages hard to read; they wanted the console style kept and no explanatory lines.

**How to apply:** keep the groups and the folding when adding a menu or a section; put what a menu holds in its `about`, never as a line under its name; don't add intro paragraphs to pages. Related: [project-world-engine](project-world-engine.md), [user-no-coding-experience](user-no-coding-experience.md).

## The character sheet became a printout (2026-11-08)

They asked for a sci-fi character sheet "in the style of the game" and saw five examples on one page (A console dossier, B paper crew record, C bracketed pane with an attribute octagon, D body and gear, E amber registry printout). They picked E and asked that it "slides open and doesn't take a lot of the screen. It should open inside the map part and not be a menu on the right."

What went in: `public/sheet.js` (its styles inside it, so `style.css` wasn't touched). The Character entry in `MENUS` has `window: "sheet"`, so `openMenu` in `public/app.js` toggles the printout instead of opening the drawer; C toggles it, Esc or `[ TEAR OFF ]` sends it back. In a room it's drawn inside `.walk-map` (`app.sheet` in `walkViewNode`), top right under the clock, `min(62ch)` wide. The same day they asked for no scroll bar, "everything is shown": `placeSheet` keeps it below the quest tracker where they'd touch and above the "What do you do?" window (`#pop-row`), shrinks its type from 12px until the whole record fits, and when that would go under 10px prints it in two columns (`.two`) if that leaves bigger type; it places itself again when the map, the tracker or that window change size (a `ResizeObserver`). All skills are listed, and FROM holds homeworld and upbringing on one line. At 1400 wide it was one column at 10px, at 810 two columns at 11.5px, nothing hidden or overlapping. Without a room it floats at the top right of the screen. The paper feeds down in 16 steps (`steps()`), and a redraw mid-feed carries on from where it was; a timer takes the animation off after it should have finished, because a hidden window doesn't play animations. It prints everything the old Character menu showed (and skills, traits with the bad ones in red, gear and credits), keeps its buttons (raise an attribute, work, sleep), and links to Skills, Perks and the inventory. A fight, check, talk or computer closes it. The drawer starts on Skills now (`ui.tab`), since the narrow window's Menu button opened `ui.tab`. Checked at 1400 and 810 wide in a test copy with a stand-in folder and no AI; the slide itself wasn't seen because the browser pane was hidden. Not played by them yet.

**How to apply:** the old `characterMenu` in `public/menus.js` is still there but no longer opened; new things about the player go on the printout, kept short enough that it stays a corner of the map.

## Everyone has a printout (2026-11-08)

They asked to "integrate the character sheet to all npc's, add the option to open it with the right click menu". **Registry** is on the right-click menu on anyone and in a person's window's Actions (`registry` in `public/walk.js`, nobody walks over for it). It calls `/api/person-sheet`, read-only (`personSheet` in `src/person-sheet.js`, `personHere` in `src/walk.js` for who's in the room, or anyone with a file by id), and `openPersonSheet` in `public/sheet.js` prints it on the same paper, with the same fitting. A person with a file prints their name, aliases, species, pronouns and age, post (`toOutsider`), faction, where they're based, status and attitude if met, attributes, skills, looks, their fight numbers, their pocket's difficulty, what they're doing, and their In a line as REMARKS; never goals or secrets. The crowd (a name and no file) get a record seeded from the name: species by share, attributes 2 to 4 with the species' nudges, a few skills, the job from the name's first words, and, so nothing contradicts the rules, the fight of their stand-in enemy from `world/deeds.json` and an Awareness that matches their pocket (0 or 1 easy, 2 or 3 for the watchful); REMARKS says it's from the general roll. These made-up numbers aren't used by any rule. `test/person-sheet.test.js`. Checked at 1400 (one column, 12px) and 810 (two columns, 12px) with Voss and Cook Imre.

## Scrolling with no bar, and a spring at the ends (2026-11-10)

They said "All of the menu, if they do not have enough space should be scrollable but no srcoll bar should appear or take place in the ui. Plus the menu once at the end or start should do like the apple product and continue for just a bit and come back like a spring". I took "the menu" to be the rail and the open menu with everything in it, and left the other windows (the story, the talk log, the check and computer windows, What's here, the quest log, the inventory, the Story board) as they were, to be asked about. `public/scroll.css` hides the scroll bars of `.rail`, `.drawer` and everything inside the drawer (`scrollbar-width: none` and `::-webkit-scrollbar`), so none takes up room. `public/spring.js` does the wheel's scrolling in the rail (`#rail`) and the open menu (`#side`) itself, since the browser can't go past the end: the wheel moves a goal, the scroll follows it on a critically damped spring (`FOLLOW` 24, worked out exactly per frame by `spring`, so a long frame can't set it shaking), and what goes past the end moves the part's children by `--spring` (`.springing > * { translate }`), held back harder the further it goes (`band`, at most about 90 pixels, less on a short part, and the wheel pulls half as hard once past), until the wheel has rested 0.11 s of the spring's own time and it comes back (`RETURN` 12, about half a second). Moving the children rather than the part keeps the rail's background and line in place, and moving them up doesn't shrink what the part can scroll (checked). Only a part that has more than it can show moves; a box someone types in keeps the browser's scrolling; the wheel over the menu's title scrolls the menu; Ctrl and sideways wheels are left alone; "reduce motion" turns the stretch off. Both zones are drawn afresh often, so a `MutationObserver` wires each new one, carries a spring under way into the new part (found again by a key from the nearest id), and keeps the rail where it was scrolled to, which before this went back to the top whenever the page redrew it. The menu's own place was already kept by `renderMenus`. Checked on a test copy (port 3299) at 1400 by 500 and 810 by 600: no bar and no room for one, the Ship menu stretching at the top and the bottom, the rail stretching and kept across a redraw, a redraw mid-spring carrying on, and the curve frame by frame at 60 a second (59 pixels for three notches, back in about 0.6 s). The browser pane was hidden, so the real wheel's frames came about once a second and the curves were measured by stepping the frames by hand. Untried by them, and not in the app until it's closed and opened.

Asked whether the other windows should lose their bars and spring too, they said "Yes, all windows", the same day. `scroll.css` now hides every scroll bar (`*`), and `spring.js` listens for the wheel on the whole page instead of on the two zones, after every other handler, so anything that takes the wheel for itself goes first (the room's zoom calls `preventDefault`, and the spring leaves such a wheel alone; so it does a Shift or Ctrl wheel, a sideways one, and a box someone types in). The innermost part under the mouse that has more than it shows scrolls, as the browser would choose; the menu's title still scrolls the menu. A part that holds its words straight, with no pieces of its own to move (the file on the Story board, a `<pre>`), is stretched by its own padding instead (`pad`: more at the top, or more at the bottom and scrolling into it, put back as it was afterwards), since words alone can't be moved; everything else moves its children as before (`move`). A spring's state is forgotten once it settles, so nothing builds up. The page's own scrolling (the story following new words, the talk and check logs going to their last line, the text room keeping the player in view) still works: the spring takes up from wherever the page put it. Checked on a test copy (port 3293, its own data folder) at 1400 by 420 or 500 and 810 by 600, each part stretching at the top and the bottom and coming back, with no bar and no room for one: What's here, the rail, the quest log's two sides, the inventory's grid, a talk's choices, the Story board's boxes, its inspector and its file, the classic view's story, the hacking practice's desk and console log, and the new game's two columns. Not checked part by part: a fight's window, a computer's screens, a thing's window and a long speech pane, which go through the same code.

## The old rail back (2026-11-10)

On 2026-11-10 they said "Bring back the old menu for the further most right menu", the day after the strip below went in and before they'd said anything else about it. I took "the old menu" to be the rail as it was before the strip, and left What's here floating over the room, since it isn't a menu and they'd chosen it. So the rail is back exactly as it was (the commit before a31782e): 124 wide, every menu its own button under its group's heading (`railNode` draws `menuButtons`, the same buttons as the drawer's tabs), scrolling if the window's short, and hidden at 860 wide or less, where the top bar's Menu button and the drawer's tabs take over. `MENU_GROUPS` lost `short` and `icon`, and `iconNode`, `ui.railOpen`, `shutRail`, the capture-phase Esc and click listeners and the `.rail-slot`, `.rail-group-button`, `.rail-dot` and `.rail-flyout` styles went. 842 tests, and the rail, a menu opened from it, What's here beside it and the narrow window's Menu were looked at in the browser pane at 1400 and 810 wide on a test copy (port 3299). Untried by them.

## The strip (2026-11-09, replaced by the old rail on 2026-11-10)

They asked to "remodel the interface, espacialy the one on the right", in "a special app". I offered an editor built into the game (my pick) or Claude's design canvas, and they chose the canvas: the screen as it was and three ideas for the right side (one column; menus along the top; floating over the room). Their only edit to the third was deleting the help line at the bottom right, and they said "You can integrate C but without the things subcategory". So the rail became a strip 60 wide (`--rail-width: 60px`): `railNode` in `public/app.js` draws a button for each group in `MENU_GROUPS`, which gained `short` (You, Story, Ship, Gear, Game) and `icon` (a small line drawing as SVG shapes, drawn by `iconNode`), with a dot when one of its entries' alerts is up. A click opens the group's menus beside the strip, over everything including an open menu (`ui.railOpen`, `.rail-flyout`, with a `-- You --` head and the entries' badges); picking one opens it through `toggleMenu`; a click anywhere else or Esc puts them away (`shutRail`, both listened for in the capture phase so nothing else acts on them). The open menu's group stays lit, and a group's tooltip lists its menus, keeping to their rule of no explaining text on screen. The strip shows down to 561 wide, where the top bar's Menu button and the drawer's tabs take over (`menuButtons` still draws those); they used to from 860, because the old rail was 124 wide. On a narrow window the drawer stops at the strip. Checked with `strip-check.mjs` (scratchpad) at 1384, 814, 620 and 540 wide. Untried by them.
