# project-tiled

*The pre-rendered direction they chose on 2026-11-08: rooms drawn in Tiled first (src/tiled.js, the same day), then the ship redrawn as three continuous decks at their 'One map per deck' (deck1/2/3.tmj, rooms as rectangles on a deck, laid out after the Anvil Carrack as a starting point for them to redraw), then their own pictures with the game's lighting, then a Blender camera setup; untried by them*

After the quest system (2026-11-06) they asked, verbatim: "I want to design the ship's map myself, what would I need to do so? Would it be easily put in the game? Is there some limits of what I can do because of the coding language?", then "Is there a program that would allow me to build the map with asset myself, what are they and how much would it cost?", then "What would be the best for a sci-fi  isometric game like colony ship?". I recommended pre-rendered art: pieces modelled in Blender, rendered flat from the game's angle, laid out in Tiled and drawn by the game, with Tiled and Blender free, Quaternius's Modular Sci-Fi MegaKit (CC0) to start from, licences that allow a public copy, no AI art, and characters as the hard part. I offered three steps: (1) the game reads Tiled's map files, (2) their pictures drawn with the game's lighting in place of its blocks, (3) a camera setup for Blender matching the game's angle and tile size. They answered "Pre-rendered" (2026-11-08), over real 3D like Colony Ship.

Step 1 was built on 2026-11-08, untried by them:
- **The catch:** Tiled's isometric grid only has square tiles, and the game's squares are half as wide as they are deep (they started as letters). So a Tiled tile is one square unit of the isometric view, two game squares side by side, and a tile's `mark` is one character or two (a thin wall is `#.`), with `floor` saying which are floor. That let every one of the 122 rooms come back exactly. Tiles are 128 by 64, chosen with step 3's renders in mind.
- **In a map file** (`world/maps/<place>/<name>.tmj` or `.tmx`): layers Floor and Walls and things (the top tile counts), People and things (tile objects from the stand-in tileset, standing where their feet are), Words (text objects, class Writing or Label), and, since the decks, Rooms (rectangles of class Room); the map's name, aliases, desc, floor and colours are map properties. `mark` on an object keeps its letter, so thing ids and `via` stay the same; `via` pairs itself when missing; hidden layers are left out.
- **The game** (`src/tiled.js`, `drawPlace` and `roomsRedrawn` in `src/world.js`, `redrawnRooms` and `onFloor` in `src/walk.js`): a Tiled map replaces the place file's room in `WORLD_MAPS`; files are looked at once a second and read again on a change, the maps, systems and lives made again, the picture redrawn, and the player moved off a new wall. What's wrong goes to `MAP_FILE_PROBLEMS` and `npm run check-story`, and never stops the game. `node tools/to-tiled.mjs <place>` moves a place's rooms into Tiled one to a file; the stand-in tileset (`world/maps/tiles/stand-ins.tsj`, pictures by `tools/stand-in-art.mjs`) only ever grows, since maps know tiles by number. The other 23 places are still JSON.

**The ship became three decks the same day,** at their "One map per deck": they'd asked "I want to redo the map from of the ship the from the ground up. First could you inspire the map of the ship on the Anvil Carrack from https://maps.adi.sc/?ship=carrack&deck=1. Could the map be all connected without loading and teleportation, so it must be continuous? If so, could you show me exemple of the map in tiles?" I showed four pictures and offered A (the whole ship as one map) or B (one map per deck, instant stairs, my pick); they chose B.
- **The content:** `deck1.tmj` command (bridge, Names Bulkhead, chart room, comms, six officers' cabins with the Speaker's at the end, wardroom, quiet room, heads, boat bay, two stairs), `deck2.tmj` civilian (medbay, main passage, mess, galley, garden, two airlocks, armory, old brig, hot bunks, overflow, two stairs), `deck3.tmj` engineering (crawlways, keel passage, stores, repair, life support, aft holds, lighter bay, pumps, engineering, one stair), 72 by 32 tiles each, built by a scratch script (`ship-build.mjs`, not in the repository) that carried the old rooms' people, things, words and descriptions over by scaling and re-placed every spot in `world/life/meridian.json`. It's a starting point for them to redraw. Old room ids kept. The stair rooms are named apart (Upper/Lower Forward Stair, Upper/Middle/Lower Aft Stair) because the story finds rooms by name. `meridian.json` keeps the name, the start and the old `overview` only for saves from when the ship was one plan.
- **The engine:** a room is an area on a deck (`compileAreas` in `src/walk.js`: start, `roomAt`, `doorAreas` with the deck itself as a side and internal doors left alone), `map.rooms` indexes every room by id (`roomOf`, `roomsOf`, `roomsOn`, `roomHere`), so stories, quests, systems, days and computers kept working by id; `go room:<id>` walks or takes the stairs; a room's lock shuts the doors in its walls as inner exits named from the player's side; `personWhere`/`bringOver`/`goOn` treat "here" as the same room; the deck plan is made from the decks with a tab per deck; the text view scrolls to keep the player in sight; the iso view darkens rooms one by one; the page keeps the deck as its level (`levelIdOf` in `public/move.js`). Size cap 100 by 60 tiles.
- **Tested:** 843 tests (`test/decks.test.js` new), the browser check `decks-check.mjs` (scratch) 38 of 38 at 1384 and 814 wide with no page errors, `check-story` clean. **Not checked:** opening the files in Tiled itself (not installed here); the real AI hasn't run any of it; the owner hasn't seen it.
- **Their first reports, the same day: "When I zoom and dezoom, it creates a huge lag"**, then "The game is not fluid when moving and when you zoom and dezoom fast, the map desapear for a moment." The whole deck's still picture was drawn afresh at every notch of the wheel, each block's shadow blurred on its own. Then "Could you make it so that the map is always generated in it's entirety so make it seamless and doesn't regenerate texture when zooming." Settled in `public/iso.js`: the whole level is drawn once per scene at its rest size (within 40 Mpx), in 256 px tiles a few a frame, the view first then nearest-first until whole, and zooming only scales it (a little soft zoomed in, which they asked for over regeneration); a small whole-level picture (`farPicture`) and the level's picture from before show under gaps, so nothing goes blank; the floor is pattern fills, shadows blurred once per tile at half size, materials once per scene, per-frame effects culled to the view. Headless Edge, up to 1920 wide at pixel ratio 1.5: every frame 7 ms walking, zooming fast, and even walking while the deck is still being filled in. Lessons: measure with the raster forced, since canvas work is deferred; a screenshot in the measured window fakes a 500 ms stall; they prefer a stable picture over a sharp one.
- **The ship's look (2026-11-08):** they asked "how can you make it look more like a spaceship with the tiles graphic engine?" and, offered example pictures, "show me three examples but with three rooms (bridge, engineering and mess hall)". Built for real in `public/iso.js`, not mocked: a place whose look names a `style` (`SHIP_STYLES`: navy grey, worn industrial, clean warship) gets a hull skin with frames outside its walls, bulkhead door frames, pipes and light strips along the walls, passage floors, and tinted walls/floors/light. Three stacked pictures went to them and they picked "Navy grey" (2026-11-08): `world/looks.json` has `style: "navy"` on the Meridian, the other two stay in `SHIP_STYLES`. Tweaks to it are theirs to ask for; after that the pre-rendered step 2.

**A test room in Blender (2026-11-12),** after they asked whether the map could be rebuilt in Blender with an asset pack. Offered Quaternius's Modular Sci-Fi MegaKit (my pick, closer to the navy grey) or Kenney's Space Station Kit (every piece needed, bright and cartoony), they answered "Kenney kit". It's in `art/kenney-space-station-kit/` (CC0, version 1.0, the 90 .glb models, their palette and the licence; `SOURCE.txt` says where from). `tools/blender-room.py` builds one Room of a deck out of it in Blender 5.2, headless (how to run it is at its top), and changes nothing in the game:
- **The map** is read by `src/tiled.js` through Node, so it's the room the game plays. One kit unit is one tile. Whole tiles get the panelled floor plate, half tiles the plain one squeezed; walls are panels along the floor's outline, facing the floor, with a door piece where the square behind is a door; a zigzag of short steps (the bow) becomes one slanted wall through their middles; walls the game cuts low (`hides` in `public/iso.js`, for a slanted wall by which way it faces) are plain blocks, with a gap for a door (all four of the bridge's doors are in low walls, so it shows no door piece); high walls get a block behind their panels for a top; a row of machine squares is a bank of consoles; things are matched to pieces by their names (`THINGS`), and seats face the viewport; people aren't built.
- **The camera** is orthographic, 30 degrees down and 45 round. `project` in `public/iso.js` draws heights at z/2, shorter than a true camera from that angle would, so the room is squashed upwards by sqrt(2/3): a tile comes out 128 by 64 and the walls 1.7 high, as in the game. `--tint` repaints the kit's greys navy and leaves its screens amber.
- **Gotchas:** the glTF importer leaves objects turning by quaternion, so `rotation_euler` did nothing until `rotation_mode = "XYZ"`; the kit's wall panels have posts at their ends, which look like a comb when cut low; Blender's shader colours are linear, so the kit's lavender counts as 0.41 saturated there.
- **What it showed:** the shapes line up one to one with the game's own picture at the same size; the kit is built for whole tiles, so a wall one square thick is two thin panels with a block between them, a door one square wide is a kit door squeezed to half, and the slanted wall leaves small gaps where it meets straight ones; the kit is small beside the game's people (walls stretched from 1 to 1.7 high, furniture scaled 1.3, and the captain's chair is still tiny); the map has no seats at the consoles, the forward viewport is a thing standing mid-floor (made windows in the nearest wall, the top one), and the floor writing, the stars and the lamps aren't built. Deck 1 has a square of the bridge's floor touching open space at row 6, column 13 (a two-square step in the bow with no wall square), which the game doesn't mind.
- **Three decks** have 34 rooms and 34 things drawn in their maps; the script does any room by its id in about ten seconds, but the kit matches only 16 of the things, and nothing for the reactor, the kettle, the garden or deck 3's machines. For the game to use pictures like these (step 2), a whole-room picture won't do, since people walk behind walls and consoles: each piece has to be rendered on its own from this camera into a tileset (step 3) and drawn by the game, sorted with the people, under its own lighting.
- The pictures (the bridge in the kit's colours, in navy grey, and a side-by-side with the game's own view taken from a test copy on port 3297) went to them on 2026-11-12. They haven't said what they think; the next step waits for their word.

**Why:** their vision is a game like Age of Decadence and Colony Ship with an artist hired later, and they want to lay out the maps themselves; a continuous ship was their explicit ask.

**How to apply:** ask how the decks look to them before step 2 (their pictures in place of the blocks, lit by the game) and step 3 (a Blender camera at the game's angle, 2:1, and 128 by 64 tiles). Ask before downloading or installing anything for them. Moving another place into Tiled, or joining its rooms into decks, is their call.
