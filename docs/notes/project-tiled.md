# project-tiled

*The pre-rendered direction they chose on 2026-11-08: rooms drawn in Tiled first (built the same day: src/tiled.js, the Meridian's 8 rooms moved exactly, stand-in tiles, a room redrawn while the game runs), then their own pictures with the game's lighting, then a Blender camera setup; untried by them*

After the quest system (2026-11-06) they asked, verbatim: "I want to design the ship's map myself, what would I need to do so? Would it be easily put in the game? Is there some limits of what I can do because of the coding language?", then "Is there a program that would allow me to build the map with asset myself, what are they and how much would it cost?", then "What would be the best for a sci-fi  isometric game like colony ship?". I recommended pre-rendered art: pieces modelled in Blender, rendered flat from the game's angle, laid out in Tiled and drawn by the game, with Tiled and Blender free, Quaternius's Modular Sci-Fi MegaKit (CC0) to start from, licences that allow a public copy, no AI art, and characters as the hard part. I offered three steps: (1) the game reads Tiled's map files, (2) their pictures drawn with the game's lighting in place of its blocks, (3) a camera setup for Blender matching the game's angle and tile size. They answered "Pre-rendered" (2026-11-08), over real 3D like Colony Ship.

Step 1 was built on 2026-11-08, untried by them:
- **The catch:** Tiled's isometric grid only has square tiles, and the game's squares are half as wide as they are deep (they started as letters). So a Tiled tile is one square unit of the isometric view, two game squares side by side, and a tile's `mark` is one character or two (a thin wall is `#.`), with `floor` saying which are floor. That let every one of the 122 rooms come back exactly; whole tiles alone would have reshaped the ship (166 mixed pairs in the bunks alone). Tiles are 128 by 64, chosen with step 3's renders in mind.
- **In a room file** (`world/maps/<place>/<room>.tmj` or `.tmx`): layers Floor and Walls and things (the top tile counts), People and things (tile objects from the stand-in tileset, standing where their feet are), and Words (text objects, class Writing or Label); the room's name, aliases, desc, floor and colours are map properties. `mark` on an object keeps its letter, so thing ids and `via` stay the same; `via` pairs itself when missing; a way out named after a room needs no `to`; hidden layers are left out.
- **The game** (`src/tiled.js`, `drawPlace` and `roomsRedrawn` in `src/world.js`, `redrawnRooms` and `onFloor` in `src/walk.js`): a Tiled room replaces the place file's room in `WORLD_MAPS`; files are looked at once a second and read again on a change, the maps, systems and lives made again, the picture redrawn (`level.drawn`, the tick's `drawn`), and the player moved off a new wall. What's wrong goes to `MAP_FILE_PROBLEMS` and `npm run check-story`, and never stops the game.
- **The Meridian's 8 rooms** were moved by `node tools/to-tiled.mjs meridian`, which reads each room back before writing anything and never writes over a room file. The stand-in tileset (`world/maps/tiles/stand-ins.tsj`, 4 markers and 87 pieces, pictures drawn by `tools/stand-in-art.mjs` with no other program) only ever grows, since maps know tiles by number. `world/maps/the-last-meridian.tiled-project` lists the folders and gives each kind of object its boxes. The deck plan stays in `meridian.json`, drawn in characters, and the other 23 places are still JSON, one command each.
- **Tested:** `test/tiled.test.js` (8, including all 122 rooms round-tripping, a .tmx, a live change and the tool on sarn), 832 tests in all; a browser check where crates drawn into bridge.tmj appeared by themselves at 1384 and 814 wide with no errors; their autosave (at Haven) opens unchanged. **Not checked:** opening the files in Tiled itself, which isn't installed here; the files follow Tiled's documented format.

**Why:** their vision is a game like Age of Decadence and Colony Ship with an artist hired later, and they want to lay out the maps themselves. Pre-rendered keeps the game's own drawing, lighting and systems.

**How to apply:** ask how Tiled went before step 2 (their pictures in place of the blocks, lit by the game) and step 3 (a Blender camera at the game's angle, 2:1, and 128 by 64 tiles). Ask before downloading or installing anything for them. Moving another place into Tiled is their call.
