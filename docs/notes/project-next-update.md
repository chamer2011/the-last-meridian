# project-next-update

*The owner's big update after 0.3.0 (announced 2026-11-05): three points agreed and on a plan page; all three are built (hacking, a usable system on every computer, each place's own systems) and untried by them*

On 2026-11-05, after release 0.3.0 and the licence switch, the owner said the next step "will be the most consequential update for now and will need a comprehensive list of the step", with "3 crucial point I want to be implemented", and asked me to describe how I'd do each before building. They want the whole update laid out as steps first; I offered to write it up as a page they can keep and tick off once all three are described.

**Point 1, agreed ("Yes, point 2" after the description).** Their words: "A big change on how the game deal with the skill Hacking. The skill window feels right now static and unfun to play with, it should be difficult to hack something depending on the importance of the computer. And there shouldn't have a percentage to click on to confirm the hack as it cancel the hacking mini-game. There should be a side window sliding on the right giving hints to win the hacking mini-game depending on the level of the skill." What I described and they accepted:
- The difficulty word a scene or the AI already gives (`[hacking:hard]`, the `skill_check` tool's `difficulty`) becomes a class of computer shown in the window's title: door panel (easy), crew terminal (moderate), ship's main system (hard), Ministry or Navy server (very hard), the Beacon or the Choir (nearly impossible). The class sets the password's length, the dump's size, the trace's patience, the decoys, and the layers (two passwords from hard, three for the worst). The classes and their numbers live in `world/checks.json`.
- No odds bar and no Execute for hacking: cracking the password is success, the trace completing is failure, and a Disconnect command is a quiet failure. Skill matters through the tools by tier and the trace's allowance. "Just a roll" in Settings still skips the game; Practice gets a class picker.
- A hint window slides in from the right of the check window, titled with the training level, updating after every move: untrained general advice; novice how many words still fit; trained which words fit and which file to read; expert the best next word and a warning before a watched file; master all that plus the tries left. It only advises. Its text lives in `world/checks.json` by tier. Perks and gear that raise the window tier raise the hints.
- A completed trace on a hard class or worse puts a fact on the record (such as `traced_on_ministry_server`) for stories to react to.
- The AI's tool list and instructions don't change, so no reload. The numbers get tuned by simulating each class against each tier thousands of times, so a trained hacker on a crew terminal gets in about as often as the old roll gave.
- Steps: classes in checks.json and the title; the game (layers, decoys, decided ending, Disconnect, the record mark); the hint window; tuning by simulation with a table for them; tests and a browser check at both widths; README, scene how-to and notes; their test, then the public copy when they say.
- Other skills' windows stay as they are unless they ask afterwards.

**Point 2, agreed ("Yes, point 3" after the description).** Their words: "now I want the player to be able to interact with the computer once it's hacked. That means that I want you to generate a operating system that the player can interact with." What I described and they accepted:
- Getting in: a successful hack doesn't close the console; ACCESS GRANTED and the screen becomes the computer's own system. The Meridian's own computers are the player's with no hack; a board, console or panel in a room that's Used logs them in if it's theirs, else asks for a password; a password learned in a letter, file or talk gets in without the hack (`* password <computer> <word>` or similar, so the written story can hand out keys).
- The system: console style, all clickable: a left column with Files, Mail, Logs, Programs, Network; the middle shows what's opened; a command line at the bottom for typing if wanted; point 1's hint window stays on the right and advises about the computer (what to read first, what a program would do, how much room before the trace).
- Contents: files (manifests, notes, records), mail (letters between people, names link to lore), logs built from the living world's data (a dock's berth log of the fleet's ships, a market terminal's prices, a ward's power board's lights and doors), programs that do real things (open a door, cut cameras, unlock a crate, change a record, move credits, send a message as someone else, shut a system down), network (other computers reachable from this one, each needing a hack of its own class).
- Effects through the written story's own effect code (door on the map, item, credits, quest, fact on the record, standing); what's read goes into What you know; one `[COMPUTER]` line in the story at log-off summing the session; facts stay on the record for stories and the AI.
- The trace inside: on a hacked computer each action can raise it, faster by class; complete = thrown out + the mark on the record; none on the player's own computers. The clock runs a few minutes per action; the fleet's orders wait until log-off, as in a talk.
- Computers are plain text files `world/computers/<id>.txt` (header: name, class, owner, where, flavor; sections Files, Mail, Logs, Programs, Network, with the scene grammar's conditions and effects on programs), checked by check-story, shown on the Story board (Computers in the picker). Every board/console/panel in the rooms and every door that asks for a hack gets a generic computer of its class built from the world's data, with editable flavor by class and faction in a file like checks.json. Rooms have 350 things, 28 computer-like by name, 6 with a written result today.
- The AI untouched: its hacking checks land on the place's computer of that class or a generic one, and it hears one line. No reload, nothing costs anything.
- Steps: the file format, how-to, checker, generic computers and flavor file; the system window with the hint window and the trace inside; effects and results (effect code, What you know, log-off line, facts, clock); the ways in (after a hack, Use on a thing, a learned password, the AI's checks; maps' things and doors get a `computer` field); the Story board's Computers; content: the Meridian's own computers and one example per class as marked drafts for them to edit or replace; tests, browser checks at both widths, README, how-to, notes; their test, then the public copy when they say. Everything on the computers is theirs to write.

**Point 3, agreed ("Yes, write the plan" after the description).** Their words: "the point 3 will be the more complex. I want you to connect the computers to an electric system over the whole local map (not the systems altogether). So each map as it's own centralised computer system that control every part of the map. For example, a computer in the security room would control the alarm and camera, a computer in the bridge would have acces to logs and navigation, a computer in the engenering would control life support and other important system of the ship/station/map. I will want to expend the scope even more when I expend the maps wich are quite small right know, so could you make it easy for you to had more computer to the system and what they control. All of the systems that the computers control should exist in the world, life support having aeration shaft in each room for exemple". What I described and they accepted:
- Each place (24 maps, 122 rooms, 15 story doors, 62 people's homes) gets its own systems, none reaching another place's: power (a junction box per section), life support (a vent in every room), lights (the rooms' lamps), cameras (a box with a red light, in listed rooms), alarm (a speaker), comms (a console), engines and navigation on ships, docking on stations, plus whatever they add. Every part is a thing in a room with a look line and a visible state, drawn with its own small drawing (today all things are one crate shape in `drawThing`), and workable by hand through the existing check windows: cut or repair a junction box (Engineering), cover a camera (Stealth), seal a vent, force a door panel.
- Computers (point 2) control systems by room: a security console has cameras and alarm, a bridge the logs, navigation and its deck's doors, engineering power, life support, lights, engines; a core computer everything at the hardest class. What a computer controls becomes its Programs automatically.
- Effects: power off darkens a section, stops its door panels, computers, cameras and later its vents; air off makes a room's air go bad over minutes, people leave for a room with air, the player takes stress then harm, someone asleep is in danger; doors lock/unlock/open and walking obeys; cameras on means acts are seen (trace, alarm), off means Stealth is easier; the alarm ringing sends the place's guards (named in the file, else people with a watch in their day) to the room, locks the doors the file says, costs faction standing if seen, gives people talk, stops from the security computer or after a while; comms jammed means nobody comes; dark rooms help Stealth and hurt Awareness; the place's engineers repair a broken part over time, or the player does. The Meridian has all of it with no hacking, shown in the Fleet menu; raids damaging her systems is room for later.
- One text file per place, `world/systems/<place>.txt`, with a how-to and the checker: lines like `vent in every room`, `camera in The Docks, The Customs Hall`, `junction box for The Docks and The Warrens in The Sump`, `computer watch_console in The Watch Room: cameras, alarm`, `computer core in The Stacks: everything, class very hard`, `guards: the night watch`, `engineers: Dace`. Places without a file get defaults (power, air, lights, doors everywhere, a camera at the way in, an alarm, a computer per existing board or console). A new room gets its vent and lights by itself; the Story board shows a place's systems as boxes linked to rooms, parts and computers.
- State in the save per place, read by walking, checks, people's days (life.js), the story (lines, facts). Scene grammar words: `? power off here`, `? alarm ringing at haven`, `* cameras off in The Docks`. The AI gets one snapshot line about the place's systems and can't flip them (no tool change, no reload). The 15 story-only doors stay the story's unless a file names them.
- Steps: the file, how-to, defaults, checker, state; the parts (drawings, looks, states, placing, by-hand work); the effects; computers' programs from what they control; the scene words and the AI's line; the Meridian's systems and the Fleet menu; the Story board's Systems; content for the Meridian, one station and one settlement as marked drafts; tests, browser checks, README, how-to, notes; their test, then the public copy when they say.
- Numbers (how long bad air takes, dark-room bonuses, how long an alarm rings) start as my guesses and live in the files to tune.
