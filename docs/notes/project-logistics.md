- Steps, each after their test: (1) stores you can see and open + Ship menu Stores line; (2) machines on the clock and everyone eating (the whole 900 aboard, a crate = 200 sittings); (3) errands: people carry, stand waiting when a source is empty; (4) the generator: supply 100 vs loads (air 20, engines 30 under way, doors 6, comms 6, nav 6, cameras 4, lights 2 a room, computers 2), drop order cameras, comms, lights, doors, nav, air, the reactor gauges program; (5) the boundary: shuttle stores refilled every 6 hours from nothing, ration crates and fuel cells both, as they first said (the fleet went on 2026-11-08, so there are no fleet holds to draw on; the Ship menu's stores and the pantry then read the same count), a manifest program in the lighter bay; (6) story conditions/effects (`? stock`, `* stock`, `* refill`, facts) and the AI's line; (7) later: other places, real trade replacing the refills, parts for repairs.

## The rework: way 3, planned 2026-11-15, step 1 built 2026-11-16

On 2026-11-15 they asked, verbatim: "Would there be a more sotisphicated/better way to deal with logistic than the flowchart that is implemented, for the system/engine of the game?" Three ways were laid out in chat (1, smarten the written routes; 2, needs and offers with one work board, my pick; 3, the same reaching the ports, one economy for the whole map) and they answered "Do way 3". I had defined way 3 as "I plan way 2 with the ports as its later steps", so it was taken as: plan it, and build nothing until they name a step.

**The plan page:** https://claude.ai/artifact/DzDRVJMrK8pJ9jT18zsiue (Logistics Rework Plan). Its source lives in the artifact only. Its ticks and their choices are in the page's own database, the document `plan/state`: `{ steps: { <id>: true }, choices: { <id>: <option id> } }`, read with ArtifactData. It takes over the old plan's unbuilt steps 6 and 7, and says so.

**What was found in the code that day, which is why a rework and not a patch:**
- An errand has one `from` and one `to`; `runErrands` leaves its carrier `waiting` at an empty source.
- Only five items count for the ship (`STORE_ITEMS` in `src/ship.js`, the galley's `takes`): ration crates, produce, medicine crates, parts crates, fuel cells. The loot's 35 foods and 25 medicines never help it; a tin put in the pantry is never cooked.
- Six senders pull people off their day, each choosing its own person, in an order fixed in `detoursFor` (`src/life.js`): `medbayDetour` (the fallen and who carries them), the systems' `detourFor` (alarm, repairs, bad air), `detourExtras` (walking with the player, brawls), `errandDetour`, `workshopDetour`, `queueDetour`. An errand counts its carrier as arrived after `WALK_MINUTES` (15) whatever they're doing, `freeHand` only looks at other errands, and `detourFor` hands repairs to `engineers[i % n]` without looking at errands: a tech can be on both at once.
- Every new use got a header line and code of its own (`a repair takes`, `salvage makes`), and nine files besides `src/bag.js` and `src/logistics.js` reach into the stores (emergencies, law, making, medbay, rig, ship-parts, ship, trade, workshop).
- Only the Meridian has a logistics file. A port keeps a level for each good where 100 is normal (`supplyDay` in `src/daily.js`: it drifts by `RECOVERY` toward 100, or toward the average of the places it depends on, plus events' pushes and a lean); 17 places in `world/daily.json`, four goods.
- Four deckhands (their job is in The Lighter Bay, beside the shuttle stores) carry nothing, and the deck chief, who heads them, is marked on "errands left waiting" that techs and cooks carry.
- Measured over ten days of a new game with nothing touched (`measure-supplies.mjs`, scratch): the generator burns 3 cells a day, the garden gives about 6 produce, the galley cooks about 9 pots (1,800 sittings), so the refills bring about 3 ration crates and 3 fuel cells a day from nothing. Through `world/trade.txt` at Haven's prices (a crate 2 × food 6 = 12, a cell 0.5 × fuel 40 = 20) that's about 96 credits a day, 168 with no garden, against the purse's 60: restocking from the dock can't become the default without new numbers.
- 38 of the 113 test files touch the logistics.

**The ten steps, as on the page.** Aboard: (1) one work board carrying today's four errands unchanged, the only thing that sends anyone to carry, aware of the other senders so nobody is double-booked, with walks by real distance when unwatched; the Ship menu's Errands becomes Work. (2) Goods by what they're worth: `feeds:`, `doses:` and `spares:` lines on items beside `salvage:`; the galley, the medbay and repairs take kinds; the Ship menu counts all of it. (3) Stores say `wants N, urgent under M, carried by ...`; the Errands part goes; jobs are raised from any store that can spare, the nearest first, with the crates set aside; the users ask too (the generator, the galley before a sitting, the medbay, a repair). (4) The Work page under The ship (stores' has against wants, what's on its way, jobs by urgency with why one's stuck), the captain's First buttons and a plus and minus on wants, and I'll do it on any job. (5) Every kind of work on the board (repairs, carrying the fallen, treating, mending, fires, breaches, guards), the order of urgency in a file, a job dropped only for a clearly more urgent one; in three parts (repairs; the medbay and the workshop; fires, breaches and guards). (6) A job nobody can do becomes an ask, and the scenes' grammar asks about the board and changes it. The map: (7) ports hold real amounts for each good (holds, makes and uses a day; one line a port in a new file), shipments leave the supplier and arrive after the days the distance takes; the level stays, worked out from the amount; events cut what's made or block a route. (8) The ship lives off the ports: buying takes real crates, restocking from the dock becomes the supply and the refills from nothing end, the numbers set so a careful captain breaks even, and the way back for a ship with nothing. (9) Freight that's real: cargo is a port's surplus, rides in a hold that's a store, and changes the far port; the Market lists where each good is cheap and dear; the story asks about ports' amounts and changes them. (10) Later, and only if asked: a port's stores in its rooms where the player stands.

**The seven choices and my picks:** who carries (the deckhands first, then a store's own people, then spare hands); the captain's say (one thing first: Medbay, Engines, Galley, Repairs, As it comes); found food and medicine (they count for what they're worth); what goes on the board (every kind of work; the other option drops step 5); when the stores stop filling from nothing (at step 8 for every game, the test save keeping its switch); a ship with no money and no fuel (a way back: the slate at a port, a distress call in open space); how deep a port's supplies go (the four goods, each made from nothing only at named ports).

**How I'd build it, for whoever does:**
- Step 1: `src/work.js`, with `game.work[place] = { jobs, next }` and a job `{ id, kind: "carry", need, item, n, from, to, urgency, may, by, phase, since, load }`. Needs are raised by registered functions (a registry like `src/room-hooks.js`, since the logistics can't import everything). `busyWith(game, place, key)` asks the other senders (the fallen, the systems, the extras, the workshop) so the board never hands a job to someone they hold, and takes a job back when they take its carrier. One detour function takes `errandDetour`'s place in `detoursFor`'s chain. `errandsView` keeps its shape for the Ship menu. The errands' tests (`test/logistics-errands.test.js`, the duty's "errands left waiting") must pass unchanged: that is the proof that nothing looks different. The errands' state moves out of `game.logistics[place].errands`, so old saves need a migration.
- Distances: the rooms' graph of `mapFor(place)` (the systems use `neighboursOf`), so many minutes a room when the player isn't there.
- Step 2: the `ITEM` header in `public/world-text.js` gets feeds, doses and spares (keep the files' line order, as with keepsake and salvage, so each reads back exactly); `STORE_ITEMS` becomes "anything with a worth of this kind"; `pickTake` takes by kind; the medbay's `takeDoses` opens any medicine.
- Step 5 touches `src/medbay.js`, `src/workshop.js`, `src/systems.js` (`detourFor`) and `src/emergencies.js`: one sender at a time, each sender's tests passing before the next.
- Step 7: a port's level must stay readable where it is (`game.world.places[place].goods[good]`), since prices (`supplyFactor`), the news, `? supply`, troubles and requests read it; keep the amounts beside it and work the level out from them.

**Step 1, one work board carrying today's errands, is in (2026-11-16, at their "Yes, your picks"), untried by them:**
- **What it is.** `src/work.js` is now the only thing that sends anyone to carry between the stores: `runBoard` is called from the logistics' clock where `runErrands` was, `workDetour` took `errandDetour`'s place in `detoursFor` (`src/life.js`), and `workView` gives the Ship menu its lines, which are **Work** where they were Errands (`ship.work` in the public state; `public/ship.js`). The file's Errands lines are unchanged, and so is where the save keeps it: `game.logistics[place].errands`, by the errand's id, so there's no save version. A job under way is `{ phase, since, load, by, took, walk }`; one set down with its load is `{ phase: "down", by: null, load }`; a job nobody has taken is kept nowhere (it's there whenever the store is short), which is why the tests that look for no state when nobody can carry still pass.
- **Nobody in two places.** A job goes to the first of its people for whom `whyNot` is null: `lostReason` (dead, in the brig, being patched up, laid up, down, away), `handsFree`, held by something more pressing, on another job, `notAbout` (asleep, out of sight, away; new in `src/life.js`, `isAbout` is its negation). Held is `holdsAt`: the medbay's detour (down, making their way there, or helping someone down), the systems' `dutyDetour` (new: the guards while an alarm rings and an engineer in the last hour of a repair; `calledNow` and `dutyTurn` in `src/systems.js`, which `detourFor` now shares, so the two can't drift apart), and the rooms' extras (walking with the player, sent to the medbay, a brawl). Before this, an errand's person was only checked for sleep and hands when it began, and the errand then ran on a timer whatever they were doing: a tech at a repair, or Brandt on a cot, carried on paper.
- **Handing back.** Each piece of the clock, a carrier who's lost, has a hand that's no use or is held gives the job up: with no load it's simply gone (and comes up again at once for the next free hand, since the store's still short); with a load it's set down (`down`), and whoever takes it next is `carrying` straight to the store it was for. The load stays with the job, so nothing is lost or doubled.
- **Bad air.** Getting out of bad air is the one thing the systems send people for that doesn't hold a carrier, because a stopped generator means no air anywhere, and the cell that brings both back has to be carried. `detoursFor` asks the systems about anyone on a job with no room (`systems?.(key, work?.(key) ? null : room, asleep)`), so only an alarm or a repair turns them aside; before, they were drawn fleeing while the errand ran on.
- **Walks.** `walkMinutes(game, place, key, from, to, hours)` in `src/life.js`: `walkLength` counts the squares of the way through the decks with `nextStretch`, the same way anyone is walked, and keeps them in `lengths` (cleared with the life files); `standsAt` is where someone is (their square if the captain's at the place, else where their day has them); 18 squares a minute at the walker's own pace. A job's leg is over when its minutes are up, or sooner if they're watched and really there. Measured: shuttle stores to fuel rack 4.4 minutes, to the pantry 6.7, to the parts lockers 4.2, garden bins to pantry 2.4, Brandt from her post to the shuttle stores 8.6. So her trip is 10 down, 5 to load, 10 back in the clock's five-minute pieces, where it was no time at all with the captain ashore (`hasReached` said yes at once) and a flat 15 a leg over a jump of the clock aboard. A walk nobody can measure still takes 15.
- **What's different in play, as told to them.** Someone laid up, at a repair or called away no longer carries on paper. While Brandt is down nobody fetches ration crates, because the quartermaster's errand names only her post (a spare hand takes over at step 3, by their pick), and the cooks have what the garden gives. Trips take real minutes with the captain ashore. Made-up bodies matter: a bad knee (about one game in four for Brandt) makes her trip 15, 5 and 15.
- **Tests.** `test/work-board.test.js` (8): a tech at a repair isn't sent and the next goes; with every tech taken the job waits, the menu says why, and it's taken up after; called away before loading; called away with a load, set down and carried on, the count the same throughout; Brandt down; bad air; the walk's minutes, and the same two hours in one go and in five-minute pieces; a job from an older save with no `by` or `walk`. The errands' tests pass with three changes that are the step's own: the view's name (`workView`, `ship.work`), "nothing to carry" where it said "about their day" (and "nobody free: Quartermaster Brandt is asleep" at two in the morning), and two hours for two trips ashore where one hour did.
- **Left for later steps.** The workshop's mender and the medbay's morning queue come after the board in `detoursFor`, as they did after the errands, so a tech can still be mending on the workshop's clock while carrying: that's step 5. The repairs' own fetching of a parts crate stays in the systems' detour. The duty's "errands left waiting" still counts only a carrier standing at an empty store, not a job nobody is free for.
