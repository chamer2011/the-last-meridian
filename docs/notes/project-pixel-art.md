# The pixel-art test view (2026-11-15)

## What was asked

On 2026-11-15 the owner sent two itch.io packs by @pixel_Salvaje and asked how well they'd fit: Isometric Interiors ($15, 800+ pieces, a cosy home look) and Isometric Space Station ($10, dark sci-fi walls, floors, doors and a few machines). Both are hand-drawn ("No AI" badge on both pages) at the same 2:1 isometric angle as `public/iso.js`'s `project`, and neither has characters. My advice: the space station pack for walls, floors and doors, the game's own furniture kept, recoloured interiors pieces later. Offered a test view of the bridge with the space station pack once they'd bought it, they said "Yes, after I buy", then "Iv'e downloaded it, It's the last download in the download tab".

## The licence

"YOU CAN: Edit and use the asset in any commercial or non-commercial project. YOU CAN'T: Resell or distribute the asset to others." So the files are unpacked into `licensed/isospace/` (three 1024×1024 sheets: `IsoSpace_Level.png`, `IsoSpace_Assets.png`, `IsoSpace_Doors.png`), `licensed/` is in `.gitignore`, and it's in neither `tools/publish.mjs`'s `GAME` list nor `package.json`'s `build.files`, so it never reaches GitHub, the public copy or an installer for others. `test/pixel-art.test.js` checks all three. The owner's own installed app runs from `D:\Test IA`, so it finds the pack. The zip is still in their Downloads.

## What's built

- **Settings → Story view → Pixel art**, beside Isometric: `reading.view` stays `"iso"` (so nothing that asks whether the view is isometric changes), with `reading.isoArt` `"drawn"` or `"pixel"` and `reading.pixelLook` `"dark"` or `"light"` (a Pixel art look row under it), kept in `meridian-reading` with the rest, and a line when the pack isn't found.
- **`public/pixel-art.js`** loads the level sheet from `/licensed/isospace/` (`serveLicensed` in `server.js`: `.png` only, inside `licensed/`), reads each look's wall-top colour from the sheet, and draws: floors per tile (`drawPixelFloors`: every tile the stretch's floor cells are on, back to front, clipped to the cells, over a dark fill so joins don't show; plain plating and panels mostly, grilles on passages, mesh on a second kind of floor) and full-height walls (`drawPixelWall`: the game's own block with its top in the pack's colour, short of the front edge where a slab stands, and the pack's slab on each face that shows; a face along a row is half a tile, so a tile's two halves draw one whole slab when both show, else each its clipped half).
- **Geometry** (measured from the sheet, in pixels of a 128 cell): a floor tile's top face has corners (64, 56), (128, 88), (64, 120), (0, 88), 8 pixels thick under it; the slab facing down-right (cells 0–2 of the wall row) has its near foot at (40, 124), far foot (104, 92), near top (40, 36); the slab facing down-left (cells 5–7) has its left foot at (24, 92), right foot (88, 124), left top (24, 4). A wall is 88 pixels high, 1.375 tiles, and is stood up to the game's `WALL_HEIGHT` 1.7 by the three-point `mapping`, so nothing else in the room moves. Dark look: floors row 0 (8 tiles), walls row 1; light: floors row 4 (5), walls row 5.
- **Hooks in `public/iso.js`**: `pixelKey()` is the end of `sceneIdOf` and part of `wantedPicture`'s key, so turning it on or off, another look, or the sheets arriving draws the place again; `restUnitFor` returns `PIXEL_UNIT` (64, exactly half the pack's size) while it's on; `drawTile` hands its floor cells to `drawPixelFloors` and `drawBlock` a full wall to `drawPixelWall`.
- **Doors and furniture (2026-11-15, at their "Yes, do that")**: the doors between rooms on a ship (the scene's `doorways`) are the pack's (`drawPixelDoor`, every frame, open as well as shut, since the pack's door is the doorway's frame too, so `drawFrame` is skipped): the doors sheet has four rows of 8 a door (dark: down-left rows 0–1, down-right 2–3; light: down-right 4–5, down-left 6–7), pictures 0 shut amber, 1 shut green, 2 shut red, 3–8 opening, 9 open, 10–15 closing; `doorPicture` picks 1, then 3–8 by how open, or 2 when locked. A door stands in the FRONT of its doorway, flush with the walls beside it (`pixelDoorDepth`), so whoever's in the doorway is drawn behind its frame; a doorway along a row is half a tile and the door a whole tile, so a lone one is centred over the walls' edges either side and two side by side share one. A way out in a wall (`drawDoor`, `tall`) is the pack's door too, red when closed. Furniture: `ITEMS` maps the plot table (the holo table), desk (terminal, or the light control console), locker (dark server rack, or the light locker), barrel (barrel stack) and a plan's crates (big crate, dark or light) to cells of the assets sheet; each is stood on the middle of its place (`standingPoint`, from the box its pixels fill, read from the sheet at load by `readBoxes`), scaled as much as the walls are stood up (1.7 / 1.375), and turned round for a piece facing east or west. Chairs, benches, the command chair, bunks and cots stay the game's, since `drawPiece` redraws their backs and beds over the people in them every frame; so do shelves, consoles (the plan's machine banks), screens, tables, the kettle, plants and basins, which the pack has nothing like.

## The roadmap for reworking the map (2026-11-15)

Asked with the second pack: "Plus i what to reworks the map wit hthe new assets with you, could you make a roadmap for it?" It's a page they tick off, https://claude.ai/artifact/XkiLt5VoWsfXyideKJfGrV (its source is only there and in that session's scratch folder). **Nothing on it is built. Build only the step they name, each after their test of the one before.**

- **Where its ticks are kept**: the page's own database (the Artifact tool's data, collection `roadmap`, document `state`): `steps` (`s1` to `s10`, true once they've tried it and are happy), `choices` (by the ids below; a choice left out means my pick stands) and `rooms` (by the game's room ids, true once redrawn and walked through). Read it with ArtifactData before starting a step, and tick what's built for them to confirm.
- **Seven choices, my pick first**: `work` how we work on a room (`describe`: they say what the room is for, I build, they correct; `you-build`; `whole-deck`), `look` (`both`: Dark with Light for the medbay and galley; `dark`; `light`), `wood` the second pack's wood and orange pieces (`recolour` to steel and navy; `leave`; `drop`), `shapes` (`refit`: keep each room where it is and refit its walls and size to the pieces; `free`; `furniture`), `bow` (`straight` walls meeting at corners; `stepped`), `height` (`pack`: the packs' own wall height, nothing stretched; `game`), `people` (`keep` the game's drawn people until step 9; `pack`).
- **Ten steps**: (1) a Pieces catalogue in the Map editor, every picture of both packs sorted by where it belongs on a ship, each ticked on or off the ship; (2) the ticked pieces become furniture the game knows (name, floor size, in the way or not, seat, bed, container or wall fitting), in a plain-text file, with pictures in the Map editor's list and a plain stand-in shape in the Isometric view; (3) a live Pixel art picture of the room beside the Map editor's plan; (4) the look's rules applied to one room, the bridge (wall height, the bow, doors a tile wide, floors by room, Dark or Light by room, recolouring); (5) the pieces the game still draws itself: beds and bunks with people in them, console banks, low walls, stairs, wall fittings that show the ship's state; (6) deck 1, eleven rooms, after copying today's decks aside for the tests (`GAME_MAPS_DIR`, `GAME_LIFE_DIR`); (7) deck 2, thirteen rooms; (8) deck 3, ten rooms; (9) people (keep the game's, have the game draw them in a pixel style, or a character pack); (10) Pixel art as the game's own look, the stretch-to-fit workarounds gone, the ports and other places, and how a version for others carries the packs (inside the installer is allowed by the licence; loose files on GitHub never).
- **The feel of the ship (2026-11-15)**: the owner sent a picture from their Downloads (a top-down sci-fi deck, `il_fullxfull.5847869696_hiac.avif`) with "Could you inspire the reworks on this image, I do not have the right so use it just to get a feel of what the ship should be. ## How things are placed (2026-11-15, asked before step 4)

Before step 4 the owner asked: "Yes, start step 4 but first could you check on the internet how rooms are design for spaceship and how object are placed in regards of each other so that the objects don't feel weirdly placed". This is what was read and the rules taken from it. Every room of the rework is laid out by them, in this order, and the roadmap page has them in plain words ("How things are placed").

What was read:

- The IMO's guidelines on the layout of a ship's bridge (MSC/Circ.982, https://puc.overheid.nl/nsi/doc/PUC_2142_14/1): workstations by task, steering on the centre line, consoles low enough to see over (1.2 m), a clear way of 1.2 m from one side of the bridge to the other and 0.7 m between stations, 0.8 to 1 m between the front wall and the consoles, the floor in front of a station belonging to it and not to the passage, dim interiors without strong colours.
- NASA's habitability work, as far as search results give it, since the standards' own PDFs couldn't be read here (https://www.nasa.gov/reference/8-0-architecture-vol-2, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20220013669): private kept from public, noisy from quiet, dirty from clean; rooms grouped by what's done in them; stowage never in a path.
- Clearances from interior design (https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/interior-design-foundations/activity-space-clearances-interiors, https://www.officefurniture2go.com/tips/desk-space-planning.html): 75 to 90 cm behind a chair someone uses, 90 to 105 cm for a way people walk.
- Set dressing as artists for games and films teach it (https://morphic.com/3d-glossary/set-dressing, https://80.lv/articles/creating-a-gritty-industrial-cyberpunk-2077-inspired-room-in-ue5): the layout first and the dressing last, small things in clusters where someone would leave them, perfect lines broken a little, wear where hands and feet go, never the same prop twice side by side, light and contrast to lead the eye.
- Studies of control rooms (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10111-020-00654-9): the operators at the edge facing their screens, and whoever commands in the middle, where they see everyone.

The rules, in the game's own measures. A square is half a tile across and a whole tile down, about a metre by two. The camera looks from the south-east, so the far walls are the top one and the west one, and a near wall (bottom, east) hides about a tile of floor behind it.

1. **What the room is for comes first.** Every post is a station: a surface, a seat, and what the person looks at, facing it. Whoever commands sees every station. Stations that work together stand together.
2. **Then how people move.** A clear way two squares wide from every door to the middle of the room, a square free behind every seat and between neighbouring stations, nothing within a square of a doorway.
3. **Tall things against walls.** In rows, and on the far walls, where they hide nothing. Nothing taller than a table in the middle of a room or along a near wall. Nothing in front of a window or of a screen someone reads. Fittings of one kind hang at one height: cables and pipes at 1.4 to 1.45, signs and screens at 1.05, first aid at 0.95, an extinguisher at 0.6.
4. **Order.** Things line up with the room. A room that's commanded from is the same either side of its middle line. A module repeated (terminal and seat, cabinet and rack) reads as built on purpose. Bare floor is left bare: a room is full when its walls are, not its floor.
5. **Dressing last.** Small things in twos and threes where their user would leave them, personal things only at someone's own place (which is where the owner's Japanese pieces and game consoles go), the room's light cold and a station's light warm. A piece with four sides for anything that has to face a way.
