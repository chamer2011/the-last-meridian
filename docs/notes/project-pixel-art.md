# The pixel-art test view (2026-11-15)

## What was asked

On 2026-11-15 the owner sent two itch.io packs by @pixel_Salvaje and asked how well they'd fit: Isometric Interiors ($15, 800+ pieces, a cosy home look) and Isometric Space Station ($10, dark sci-fi walls, floors, doors and a few machines). Both are hand-drawn ("No AI" badge on both pages) at the same 2:1 isometric angle as `public/iso.js`'s `project`, and neither has characters. My advice: the space station pack for walls, floors and doors, the game's own furniture kept, recoloured interiors pieces later. Offered a test view of the bridge with the space station pack once they'd bought it, they said "Yes, after I buy", then "Iv'e downloaded it, It's the last download in the download tab".

## The licence

"YOU CAN: Edit and use the asset in any commercial or non-commercial project. YOU CAN'T: Resell or distribute the asset to others." So the files are unpacked into `licensed/isospace/` (three 1024×1024 sheets: `IsoSpace_Level.png`, `IsoSpace_Assets.png`, `IsoSpace_Doors.png`), `licensed/` is in `.gitignore`, and it's in neither `tools/publish.mjs`'s `GAME` list nor `package.json`'s `build.files`, so it never reaches GitHub, the public copy or an installer for others. `test/pixel-art.test.js` checks all three. The owner's own installed app runs from `D:\Test IA`, so it finds the pack. The zip is still in their Downloads.

## What's built

- **Settings → Story view → Pixel art**, beside Isometric: `reading.view` stays `"iso"` (so nothing that asks whether the view is isometric changes), with `reading.isoArt` `"drawn"` or `"pixel"` and `reading.pixelLook` `"dark"` or `"light"` (a Pixel art look row under it), kept in `meridian-reading` with the rest, and a line when the pack isn't found.
- **`public/pixel-art.js`** loads the level sheet from `/licensed/isospace/` (`serveLicensed` in `server.js`: `.png` only, inside `licensed/`), reads each look's wall-top colour from the sheet, and draws: floors per tile (`drawPixelFloors`: every tile the stretch's floor cells are on, back to front, clipped to the cells, over a dark fill so joins don't show; plain plating and panels mostly, grilles on passages, mesh on a second kind of floor) and full-height walls (`drawPixelWall`: the game's own block with its top in the pack's colour, short of the front edge where a slab stands, and the pack's slab on each face that shows; a face along a row is half a tile, so a tile's two halves draw one whole slab when both show, else each its clipped half).
- **Geometry** (measured from the sheet, in pixels of a 128 cell): a floor tile's top face has corners (64, 56), (128, 88), (64, 120), (0, 88), 8 pixels thick under it; the slab facing down-right (cells 0–2 of the wall row) has its near foot at (40, 124), far foot (104, 92), near top (40, 36); the slab facing down-left (cells 5–7) has its left foot at (24, 92), right foot (88, 124), left top (24, 4). A wall is 88 pixels high, 1.375 tiles, and is stood up to the game's `WALL_HEIGHT` 1.7 by the three-point `mapping`, so nothing else in the room moves. Dark look: floors row 0 (8 tiles), walls row 1; light: floors row 4 (5), walls row 5.
- **Hooks in `public/iso.js`**: `pixelKey()` is the end of `sceneIdOf` and part of `wantedPicture`'s key, so turning it on or off, another look, or the sheets arriving draws the place again; `restUnitFor` returns `PIXEL_UNIT` (64, exactly half the pack's size) while it's on; `drawTile` hands its floor cells to `drawPixelFloors` and `drawBlock` a full wall to `drawPixelWall`.
- **Doors and furniture (2026-11-15, at their "Yes, do that")**: the doors between rooms on a ship (the scene's `doorways`) are the pack's (`drawPixelDoor`, every frame, open as well as shut, since the pack's door is the doorway's frame too, so `drawFrame` is skipped): the doors sheet has four rows of 8 a door (dark: down-left rows 0–1, down-right 2–3; light: down-right 4–5, down-left 6–7), pictures 0 shut amber, 1 shut green, 2 shut red, 3–8 opening, 9 open, 10–15 closing; `doorPicture` picks 1, then 3–8 by how open, or 2 when locked. A door stands in the FRONT of its doorway, flush with the walls beside it (`pixelDoorDepth`), so whoever's in the doorway is drawn behind its frame; a doorway along a row is half a tile and the door a whole tile, so a lone one is centred over the walls' edges either side and two side by side share one. A way out in a wall (`drawDoor`, `tall`) is the pack's door too, red when closed. Furniture: `ITEMS` maps the plot table (the holo table), desk (terminal, or the light control console), locker (dark server rack, or the light locker), barrel (barrel stack) and a plan's crates (big crate, dark or light) to cells of the assets sheet; each is stood on the middle of its place (`standingPoint`, from the box its pixels fill, read from the sheet at load by `readBoxes`), scaled as much as the walls are stood up (1.7 / 1.375), and turned round for a piece facing east or west. Chairs, benches, the command chair, bunks and cots stay the game's, since `drawPiece` redraws their backs and beds over the people in them every frame; so do shelves, consoles (the plan's machine banks), screens, tables, the kettle, plants and basins, which the pack has nothing like.

## Checked

Headless Edge on a test copy (port 3297, its own data folder, the test save on the bridge): the ship loads behind the loading screen in about 0.9 s and walking keeps frames at 16.7 ms; pictures at 1400 and 810 wide in both looks; Settings at both widths; switching back to Isometric draws the game's own look at its own size. With the doors and furniture: the bridge, and the stores on deck 3 (a copy of the test save moved there through the test copy's own `/api/walk` and saved to its slot 1), at both widths, with the same load time and frames. 7 tests in `test/pixel-art.test.js`. Not seen on screen: a door sliding while someone walks through, and a locked door's red light.

## The second pack: Isometric Interiors (2026-11-15)

At their "Could you integrate the next batch of model into the game, it's the last download" the same day: `TinyHouse_0.19(@Pixel_Salvaje).zip`, 1,199 PNGs in 29 folders (Bathroom, Bedroom, Books, Carpets, Chairs, Clocks, Computer, Consoles, Desks, Doors, Floor_Wall_Tiles_32/64/128, Japanese_Room, Kitchen, Lab, Lamps, Living Roon, Office, Plants, Sofa, StairCase, Tables, Televisions_TV, Wall_Decoration, Windows and a few animations), one picture a file, 16 to 512 pixels across, drawn at the same size as the first pack (a floor tile 128 pixels wide). Unpacked to `licensed/interiors/` with its top folder taken off, and an `_about.txt` in each pack's folder saying what it is and where it came from. Same licence, same rules.

- **What's drawn from it** (`PIECES` in `public/pixel-art.js`, by the game's furniture kinds; each kind has a list of ways, the first whose pictures are in the folder winning, `pieceWay`): chair = `Chairs/Chair_4_{A,B,C,D}_Tile` (the pack's A faces the player, B right, C left, D away: the game's s, e, w, n), command-chair = `Chairs/GChair_9_{A..D}`, bench = `Tables/Table_Corridor` twice along it, table = `Tables/Office_Kitchen_Table`, long-table = the same three times along it (the orange `Table_11` looked too homely), locker = `Office/Office_Metallic_Closet_Ani/Metallic_Closet_Base` (the first pack's rack or locker standing in without the second pack), shelf = `Office/Rack`, screen = `Computer/Vertical_Screen`, kettle = `Kitchen/Oven` with `Kitchen/Pot_Full` stood 0.86 tiles up on it (the game's steam still rises over it), plant = one of four of `Plants/` by the piece's seed, basin = `Bathroom/WC_Tap_Ani/Wc_Tap_1`. The first pack keeps the plot table, desk (its terminal, dark or light), barrel and crate.
- **How**: every picture named in `PIECES` is loaded when the test is first turned on (the first pack's sheets must load; each of the second pack's files is taken if it's there, `pixelFurnitureFound` for Settings' line); `readBoxes` reads each one's solid box (`solidBox`: alpha 250 and up, since the second pack draws a half see-through shadow under its pieces); `drawPicture` stands a picture's `standingPoint` on the middle of the piece's place, scaled 1.7 / 1.375 like the first pack's, turned round for a one-sided picture facing east or west; a long piece's picture goes `times` along its length, far one first.
- **People in chairs**: the game redraws a chair's back over whoever sits facing away (the frame's `seatback` extra); in the test that redraws the chair's whole picture over them (`drawPixelPiece` there too), so they're behind the back with their head and shoulders over it. Sitting facing the player, they're drawn over the chair, as before.
- **Not taken**: bunks and cots (the game draws its sleepers under the blanket inside its own bed, `drawPiece`'s `sleepers`; the second pack has single beds and the first a bunk, for the map's rework), the wall's windows and screens (they show power), consoles (the plan's machine banks), and everything the game has no furniture kind for yet: servers, workbenches, toolboxes, cameras, switches, pipes and cables, toilets, showers, fridges, sofas, lamps, carpets, posters, stairs, 130 floors and walls. Those wait for the map's rework.
- `serveLicensed` in `server.js` now decodes its link, since the pack's folders have spaces in their names.
- **Checked** on the test copy (port 3297, its own saves; run from Bash this time, since the owner was playing in the browser pane on 3210): the bridge and wardroom, the Long Mess with someone sitting, the kettle, the galley and garden on deck 2, the heads, in the dark look at 1400 and 810 wide and the light look at 1400; loading 1.0 to 2.1 s, frames 16.7 ms. 12 tests in `test/pixel-art.test.js`.

## The roadmap for reworking the map (2026-11-15)

Asked with the second pack: "Plus i what to reworks the map wit hthe new assets with you, could you make a roadmap for it?" It's a page they tick off, https://claude.ai/artifact/XkiLt5VoWsfXyideKJfGrV (its source is only there and in that session's scratch folder). **Nothing on it is built. Build only the step they name, each after their test of the one before.**

- **Where its ticks are kept**: the page's own database (the Artifact tool's data, collection `roadmap`, document `state`): `steps` (`s1` to `s10`, true once they've tried it and are happy), `choices` (by the ids below; a choice left out means my pick stands) and `rooms` (by the game's room ids, true once redrawn and walked through). Read it with ArtifactData before starting a step, and tick what's built for them to confirm.
- **Seven choices, my pick first**: `work` how we work on a room (`describe`: they say what the room is for, I build, they correct; `you-build`; `whole-deck`), `look` (`both`: Dark with Light for the medbay and galley; `dark`; `light`), `wood` the second pack's wood and orange pieces (`recolour` to steel and navy; `leave`; `drop`), `shapes` (`refit`: keep each room where it is and refit its walls and size to the pieces; `free`; `furniture`), `bow` (`straight` walls meeting at corners; `stepped`), `height` (`pack`: the packs' own wall height, nothing stretched; `game`), `people` (`keep` the game's drawn people until step 9; `pack`).
- **Ten steps**: (1) a Pieces catalogue in the Map editor, every picture of both packs sorted by where it belongs on a ship, each ticked on or off the ship; (2) the ticked pieces become furniture the game knows (name, floor size, in the way or not, seat, bed, container or wall fitting), in a plain-text file, with pictures in the Map editor's list and a plain stand-in shape in the Isometric view; (3) a live Pixel art picture of the room beside the Map editor's plan; (4) the look's rules applied to one room, the bridge (wall height, the bow, doors a tile wide, floors by room, Dark or Light by room, recolouring); (5) the pieces the game still draws itself: beds and bunks with people in them, console banks, low walls, stairs, wall fittings that show the ship's state; (6) deck 1, eleven rooms, after copying today's decks aside for the tests (`GAME_MAPS_DIR`, `GAME_LIFE_DIR`); (7) deck 2, thirteen rooms; (8) deck 3, ten rooms; (9) people (keep the game's, have the game draw them in a pixel style, or a character pack); (10) Pixel art as the game's own look, the stretch-to-fit workarounds gone, the ports and other places, and how a version for others carries the packs (inside the installer is allowed by the licence; loose files on GitHub never).
- **The feel of the ship (2026-11-15)**: the owner sent a picture from their Downloads (a top-down sci-fi deck, `il_fullxfull.5847869696_hiac.avif`) with "Could you inspire the reworks on this image, I do not have the right so use it just to get a feel of what the ship should be. It's a high tech ship built rushed and that's been more or less well ''maintenu'' for a long time". **They don't have the rights to it: it's a mood reference only. Never copy it, trace it, put it in the repository or on a page.** What was taken from it, in words, is the roadmap page's "The feel of the ship" section, which every room is held up against: cold teal-grey steel, dim, with small warm pools of light where people work, eat and sleep, red points on machines, green and amber screens; **high tech** (a screen at every station, each big machine the heart of its room with a control desk in front, sliding doors with a status light, the plot table's glowing chart); **built in a rush** (pipes and cables in the open along walls and across floors, machines bolted in where they fitted with a rail round them, hazard tape and arrows meant to be temporary, wall panels that don't match, rooms doing two jobs); **kept going a long time** (a worn path where people walk and grime where they don't, replaced floor plates in another shade, stencilled and chalked labels, corners stacked with crates, cases and tools, personal things where people sit and sleep). The page's seven colours for it are my own: deep shadow #101b1e, cold steel #3f5d61, worn plating #8fa9a8, work lamp #f2a45c, hazard paint #e3c22f, warning light #e0483c, screen #57d39a. Steps 4 (the bridge) and 5 (pipes, cables, rails, hazard paint, replaced plates) are where it's built.
- **Their choices so far** (read from the page on 2026-11-15, before the feel section went up): `work` describe, `look` both, `wood` recolour, `shapes` refit, `bow` straight, `people` keep; `height` not answered, so my pick (the packs' own height) stands. The reference picture is dark all through, so they were asked whether the medbay and galley should be dark too, and answered: "Keep them light but a little less light as to not clash to hard with the rest". So `look` stays both, with the Light set toned down in those two rooms (dimmed and greyed toward the dark set, and worn like the rest); how much is theirs to pick at step 4, from the medbay drawn at two or three strengths beside the bridge. The page says so in the choice, in the feel section and in step 4.
- **How one room gets redone** (steps 6 to 8, under the `work` choice): they say what the room is for; I lay it out and send a picture; they change it in the Map editor or say what to change; I put people's places, keypads, cameras and stores back and run the tests; they walk through it and tick the room.
- **Promised on the page**: the Isometric view stays as it is until step 10; rooms keep their ids so stories, days and saves keep working; the Map editor's backups; the packs never go to GitHub.
- **Checked**: the page at 1200 wide (light) and 400 wide (dark) with nothing running sideways; the state document written and read back through ArtifactData. Their own clicks saving from the page weren't exercised.

## Step 1 of the roadmap: the catalogue of every piece (2026-11-15)

At their "Start step 1". Built, and untried by them.

- **What they see**: a **Pieces** button along the top of the Map editor opens a whole-screen window over it. Down the left, the places a piece can belong (Bridge and command, Quarters, Mess and galley, Medbay, Engineering and workshop, Stores and cargo, Heads and laundry, Passages, doors and stairs, Garden, Walls and what hangs on them, Floors, Small things, Odds and ends) with how many of each are on the ship, and **Everything**. In the middle, the pieces of the place chosen as cards: the picture on a mid grey, the name, `[x]` or `[ ]`, and how many pictures it has. A click on a card changes its tick, and it's saved a moment later. The **+** in a card's corner lays the piece out along the bottom: every picture it has, an **On the ship / Left out** button, where it is in the pack, and a row of the thirteen places to move it under. Along the top: **All / On the ship / Left out**, **Both packs / Space Station / Interiors**, **Undo** (sixty changes back) and **Close**; each place has **All on** and **All off**. Escape shuts what's laid out, then the window. At 900 wide or less the places go along the top.
- **What a piece is** (`src/pieces.js`): one thing, however many pictures. The first pack's three sheets are named by hand in `firstPack` (47 pieces: 13 floors, the walls each running both ways, low walls, corners, pillars, stairs and rails, the two doors of 32 frames, the 16 pieces of furniture). The second pack is read from its folders: a picture lying in a folder is a piece by its name with the side, state and frame taken off (`pieceKey`: `Chair_4_A_Tile` and `Chair_4_D_Tile` are `Chair_4`, `Board_Empty` and `Board_Full` are `Board`); a folder inside is part of the piece its name starts with (`Oven_Doors` goes with `Oven`) or a piece of its own; `Kitchen Furnitures Colors` is a piece a colour; a wall's left and right pictures are one wall; `PART_OF` says the red fridge's doors are the red fridge's (the pack spells it "Fidge"). Files that are several pictures side by side are cut up by the `SHEETS` table (the house doors' five frames, the books' and the lab machines' sixteen views, the glass door, the camera, the cleaning robot, the grandfather clock), found by a scratch script that looked for separate islands of filled pixels in all 947 pictures. Left out altogether: the pack's three advertising pictures, `Books_Title.png`, and the same floors and walls at 32 and 64 pixels. On their computer: **500 pieces, 1,345 pictures**.
- **Names**: `NAMES` has a name for the pieces I looked at closely (about 190); the rest are the pack's own name as words (`pretty`), with a colour at the end after a comma ("Towel, blue") and books and house doors by rule.
- **The first ticks** (`sortOut`, by folder and name): 309 of 500 on. On: everything of the first pack, the Lab, Computer and Plants folders, steel and plastic chairs, steel tables (and the orange-topped ones, to be recoloured at their word), the black and white kitchen units and what's on a galley's counters, the heads' fittings, a laundry's, wall fittings (pipes, cables, switches, cameras, air units, screens, vents, the fire extinguisher, the signs and charts), fifteen floors and seventeen walls that read as plating, tile or plain paint, and every small thing for desks. Off: the Japanese room, carpets, wooden beds and chairs, the house's doors, windows and staircase, the coloured kitchens, game consoles, presents, the cat, the bathtub and the duck, lava lamp and grandfather clock, the pictures and the two posters with brands on them, cola and cereal. It's a guess for them to change; nothing reads it yet.
- **Where it's kept**: `world/pieces.txt`, written in full at the first change (so their first click makes it), a line a piece under its place's name, `- on: Padded swivel chair [interiors/Chairs/GChair_9]`. Once it's there it wins over the guess for every piece it lists, so they can rename a piece or move a line by hand; a piece it doesn't list gets the guess, and a line for a piece the packs here don't have is kept and shown without a picture. `npm run check-story` names any line it can't read (`piecesProblems`, through `addChecker`; `src/walk.js` imports the file for that). **The file is their own work once it exists: never overwrite it, and commit it for them when it shows up.** `GAME_LICENSED_DIR` and `GAME_PIECES_FILE` point tests and a test copy elsewhere, and a test copy must set `GAME_PIECES_FILE`, or clicking in it writes their file.
- **The routes**: `GET /api/pieces` is the catalogue (`catalogue()`), `POST /api/pieces` with `{ set: [{ id, on, place }] }` changes ticks and places (`setPieces`), refusing an unknown piece or place before writing anything. `serveLicensed` now reads the same folder as the catalogue. A game started before this has no such route, and the window then says to close the game and open it again.
- **The page**: `public/pieces.js` and `public/pieces.css`. A picture is fetched only once its card is in sight, trimmed to what's drawn in it, and shown at whole pixels up to three times its size, or shrunk to fit. Changes wait 450 ms and are sent together, each piece as it is now. The window keeps the keys to itself while it's open.
- **Checked**: `test/pieces.test.js` (15 tests on a made-up pack of files that are only a size, in a temporary folder); the whole suite, 1,252 tests; and the real window in headless Edge at 1400 and 810 wide on a test copy, reached the real way (a saved game, the menu's Map editor, its Pieces button): 56 checks each, among them no two cards overlapping, nothing wider than the window, a tick saved and found again in the file, Undo, a piece laid out and moved, the filters, All off and its Undo, and Escape. The window's words for a game started before it existed, for no packs at all and for a piece whose pictures aren't there were tried by handing the page made-up answers. Not tried: their own running game (it was started before the route existed) and a screen with a different pixel ratio.
- **The roadmap page** (version 4 of it) marks step 1 "Ready to try", says how to reach it (close the game and open it again, then Game, Map editor, Pieces), and gives the real counts in place of "about 1,200". Its tick is left for them: it means they tried it and are happy.
- **Known**: a piece's card shows its first picture, which for a few is a part and not the whole (the fridge's is the pack's own sheet of its parts); the names of pieces I didn't look at closely are the pack's (Plant 1, Paper 3, Sticky note 4); the 47 first-pack pieces can be ticked off but the Pixel art view still draws them, since nothing reads the ticks until step 2.

## Known

- The bow's stepped walls read as a row of separate units, since every step shows its own slab.
- The low walls between the player and a room, the bunks and cots, the consoles, the hull skin outside and the people are the game's own drawing, so they don't match the packs.
- The second pack is a home's furniture: the washbasins' cabinets are wood, the stove is white, the benches' tops are orange. Recolouring them is allowed by the licence and not done.
- The packs' pieces are smaller than the places the game's own furniture was drawn for (a table of the pack is two thirds of a tile, the game's a whole one), so there are gaps round them until the map is redrawn for them.
- The pack's furniture faces one way: a piece facing north shows its front all the same.
- Zooming in scales the picture drawn at 64, so the pixels go a little soft past the rest size.
- The pack's walls are stretched about a quarter taller to the game's wall height.
