# The pixel-art test view (2026-11-15)

## What was asked

On 2026-11-15 the owner sent two itch.io packs by @pixel_Salvaje and asked how well they'd fit: Isometric Interiors ($15, 800+ pieces, a cosy home look) and Isometric Space Station ($10, dark sci-fi walls, floors, doors and a few machines). Both are hand-drawn ("No AI" badge on both pages) at the same 2:1 isometric angle as `public/iso.js`'s `project`, and neither has characters. My advice: the space station pack for walls, floors and doors, the game's own furniture kept, recoloured interiors pieces later. Offered a test view of the bridge with the space station pack once they'd bought it, they said "Yes, after I buy", then "Iv'e downloaded it, It's the last download in the download tab".

## The licence

"YOU CAN: Edit and use the asset in any commercial or non-commercial project. YOU CAN'T: Resell or distribute the asset to others." So the files are unpacked into `licensed/isospace/` (three 1024×1024 sheets: `IsoSpace_Level.png`, `IsoSpace_Assets.png`, `IsoSpace_Doors.png`), `licensed/` is in `.gitignore`, and it's in neither `tools/publish.mjs`'s `GAME` list nor `package.json`'s `build.files`, so it never reaches GitHub, the public copy or an installer for others. `test/pixel-art.test.js` checks all three. The owner's own installed app runs from `D:\Test IA`, so it finds the pack. The zip is still in their Downloads.

## What's built

- **Settings → Story view → Pixel art**, beside Isometric: `reading.view` stays `"iso"` (so nothing that asks whether the view is isometric changes), with `reading.isoArt` `"drawn"` or `"pixel"` and `reading.pixelLook` `"dark"` or `"light"` (a Pixel art look row under it), kept in `meridian-reading` with the rest, and a line when the pack isn't found.
- **`public/pixel-art.js`** loads the level sheet from `/licensed/isospace/` (`serveLicensed` in `server.js`: `.png` only, inside `licensed/`), reads each look's wall-top colour from the sheet, and draws: floors per tile (`drawPixelFloors`: every tile the stretch's floor cells are on, back to front, clipped to the cells, over a dark fill so joins don't show; plain plating and panels mostly, grilles on passages, mesh on a second kind of floor) and full-height walls (`drawPixelWall`: the game's own block with its top in the pack's colour, short of the front edge where a slab stands, and the pack's slab on each face that shows; a face along a row is half a tile, so a tile's two halves draw one whole slab when both show, else each its clipped half).
- **Geometry** (measured from the sheet, in pixels of a 128 cell): a floor tile's top face has corners (64, 56), (128, 88), (64, 120), (0, 88), 8 pixels thick under it; the slab facing down-right (cells 0–2 of the wall row) has its near foot at (40, 124), far foot (104, 92), near top (40, 36); the slab facing down-left (cells 5–7) has its left foot at (24, 92), right foot (88, 124), left top (24, 4). A wall is 88 pixels high, 1.375 tiles, and is stood up to the game's `WALL_HEIGHT` 1.7 by the three-point `mapping`, so nothing else in the room moves. Dark look: floors row 0 (8 tiles), walls row 1; light: floors row 4 (5), walls row 5.
- **Hooks in `public/iso.js`**: `pixelKey()` is the end of `sceneIdOf` and part of `wantedPicture`'s key, so turning it on or off, another look, or the sheets arriving draws the place again; `restUnitFor` returns `PIXEL_UNIT` (64, exactly half the pack's size) while it's on; `drawTile` hands its floor cells to `drawPixelFloors` and `drawBlock` a full wall to `drawPixelWall`.
- **Doors and furniture (2026-11-15, at their "Yes, do that")**: the doors between rooms on a ship (the scene's `doorways`) are the pack's (`drawPixelDoor`, every frame, open as well as shut, since the pack's door is the doorway's frame too, so `drawFrame` is skipped): the doors sheet has four rows of 8 a door (dark: down-left rows 0–1, down-right 2–3; light: down-right 4–5, down-left 6–7), pictures 0 shut amber, 1 shut green, 2 shut red, 3–8 opening, 9 open, 10–15 closing; `doorPicture` picks 1, then 3–8 by how open, or 2 when locked. A door stands in the FRONT of its doorway, flush with the walls beside it (`pixelDoorDepth`), so whoever's in the doorway is drawn behind its frame; a doorway along a row is half a tile and the door a whole tile, so a lone one is centred over the walls' edges either side and two side by side share one. A way out in a wall (`drawDoor`, `tall`) is the pack's door too, red when closed. Furniture: `ITEMS` maps the plot table (the holo table), desk (terminal, or the light control console), locker (dark server rack, or the light locker), barrel (barrel stack) and a plan's crates (big crate, dark or light) to cells of the assets sheet; each is stood on the middle of its place (`standingPoint`, from the box its pixels fill, read from the sheet at load by `readBoxes`), scaled as much as the walls are stood up (1.7 / 1.375), and turned round for a piece facing east or west. Chairs, benches, the command chair, bunks and cots stay the game's, since `drawPiece` redraws their backs and beds over the people in them every frame; so do shelves, consoles (the plan's machine banks), screens, tables, the kettle, plants and basins, which the pack has nothing like.

## Checked

Headless Edge on a test copy (port 3297, its own data folder, the test save on the bridge): the ship loads behind the loading screen in about 0.9 s and walking keeps frames at 16.7 ms; pictures at 1400 and 810 wide in both looks; Settings at both widths; switching back to Isometric draws the game's own look at its own size. With the doors and furniture: the bridge, and the stores on deck 3 (a copy of the test save moved there through the test copy's own `/api/walk` and saved to its slot 1), at both widths, with the same load time and frames. 7 tests in `test/pixel-art.test.js`. Not seen on screen: a door sliding while someone walks through, and a locked door's red light.

## The second pack: Isometric Interiors (2026-11-15)

At their "Could you integrate the next batch of model into the game, it's the last download" the same day: `TinyHouse_0.19(@Pixel_Salvaje).zip`, 1,199 PNGs in 29 folders (Bathroom, Bedroom, Books, Carpets, Chairs, Clocks, Computer, Consoles, Desks, Doors, Floor_Wall_Tiles_32/64/128, Japanese_Room, Kitchen, Lab, Lamps, Living Roon, Office, Plants, Sofa, StairCase, Tables, Televisions_TV, Wall_Decoration, Windows and a few animations), one picture a file, 16 to 512 pixels across, drawn at the same size as the first pack (a floor tile 128 pixels wide). Unpacked to `licensed/interiors/` with its top folder taken off, and an `_about.txt` in each pack's folder saying what it is and where it came from. Same licence, same rules.

- **What's drawn from it** (`PIECES` in `public/pixel-art.js`, by the game's furniture kinds; each kind has a list of ways, the first whose pictures are in the folder winning, `pieceWay`): chair = `Chairs/Chair_4_{A,B,C,D}_Tile` (the pack's A faces the player, B right, C left, D away: the game's s, e, w, n), command-chair = `Chairs/GChair_9_{A..D}`, bench = `Tables/Table_Corridor` twice along it, table = `Tables/Office_Kitchen_Table`, long-table = the same three times along it (the orange `Table_11` looked too homely), locker = `Office/Office_Metallic_Closet_Ani/Metallic_Closet_Base` (the first pack's rack or locker standing in without the second pack), shelf = `Office/Rack`, screen = `Computer/Vertical_Screen`, kettle = `Kitchen/Oven` with `Kitchen/Pot_Full` stood 0.86 tiles up on it (the game's steam still rises over it), plant = one of four of `Plants/` by the piece's seed, basin = `Bathroom/WC_Tap_Ani/Wc_Tap_1`. The first pack keeps the plot table, desk (its terminal, dark or light), barrel and crate.
- **How**: every picture named in `PIECES` is loaded when the test is first turned on (the first pack's sheets must load; each of the second pack's files is taken if it's there, `pixelFurnitureFound` for Settings' line); `readBoxes` reads each one's solid box (`solidBox`: alpha 250 and up, since the second pack draws a half see-through shadow under its pieces); `drawPicture` stands a picture's `standingPoint` on the middle of the piece's place, scaled 1.7 / 1.375 like the first pack's, turned round for a one-sided picture facing east or west; a long piece's picture goes `times` along its length, far one first.
- **People in chairs**: the game redraws a chair's back over whoever sits facing away (the frame's `seatback` extra); in the test that redraws the chair's whole picture over them (`drawPixelPiece` there too), so they're behind the back with their head and shoulders over it. Sitting facing the player, they're drawn over the chair, as before.
- **Not taken**: bunks and cots (the game draws its sleepers under the blanket inside its own bed, `drawPiece`'s `sleepers`; the second pack has single beds and the first a bunk, for the map's rework), the wall's windows and screens (they show power), consoles (the plan's machine banks), and everything the game has no furniture kind for yet: servers, workbenches, toolboxes, cameras, switches, pipes and cables, toilets, showers, fridges, sofas, lamps, carpets, posters, stairs, 130 floors and walls. Those wait for the map's rework.
- `serveLicensed` in `server.js` now decodes its link, since the pack's folders have spaces in their names.
- **Checked** on the test copy (port 3297, its own saves; run from Bash this time, since the owner was playing in the browser pane on 3210): the bridge and wardroom, the Long Mess with someone sitting, the kettle, the galley and garden on deck 2, the heads, in the dark look at 1400 and 810 wide and the light look at 1400; loading 1.0 to 2.1 s, frames 16.7 ms. 12 tests in `test/pixel-art.test.js`.

## Known

- The bow's stepped walls read as a row of separate units, since every step shows its own slab.
- The low walls between the player and a room, the bunks and cots, the consoles, the hull skin outside and the people are the game's own drawing, so they don't match the packs.
- The second pack is a home's furniture: the washbasins' cabinets are wood, the stove is white, the benches' tops are orange. Recolouring them is allowed by the licence and not done.
- The packs' pieces are smaller than the places the game's own furniture was drawn for (a table of the pack is two thirds of a tile, the game's a whole one), so there are gaps round them until the map is redrawn for them.
- The pack's furniture faces one way: a piece facing north shows its front all the same.
- Zooming in scales the picture drawn at 64, so the pixels go a little soft past the rest size.
- The pack's walls are stretched about a quarter taller to the game's wall height.
