# The ship's trade
draft: the AI's, not yet edited by the owner

# How the Meridian pays her way, now that the ports hold real amounts. Every number here is a first guess.
#
# The purse is the ship's own money, apart from yours. A levy comes into it every day, and it pays for everything the
# ship buys: her restocking, and what you buy for her by the lot from the Ship menu. What she sells, and the fees for
# cargo and passengers, go into it. Whoever has the chair has the purse.
#
# Restocking: a store whose line in world/logistics says "from the dock" is filled every so often while she's docked
# at a port that sells what it keeps. The crates and cells are real ones out of the port's own stores, at the port's
# price that day, paid from the purse. A ration crate goes by the port's food price, and a fuel cell by its fuel
# price, each times the number below. In open space nothing comes, and a port that has none sells none.
#
# The slate is the way back at a port for a ship with an empty purse: the port sells her what she needs anyway,
# dearer by the number below, up to so many credits owed there. Each midnight whatever is left in the purse goes to
# the slate, and then the day's levy comes in, so the day's needs are still bought at the usual price. Every day
# something's still owed, your standing with whoever holds the port falls. And while she owes anything anywhere, the
# dock only keeps her going: it fills her stores as far as the "while she owes" line says, not to the top.
#
# A distress call is the way back in open space, for a ship that can't get herself to any port: no fuel in the tank
# for even the shortest jump, or no fuel cells left for her generator. Sent from the Ship menu, it's answered after
# so many days by a tanker from the nearest port that sells fuel, with enough to reach that port, at the port's price
# times the number below. What the purse can't pay is owed at that port.
#
# Freight is a port's real goods going where they're wanted. While she's docked, the Market offers what this port
# can spare for a port you know of that's running low on it: so many sacks, crates or casks, which leave this port
# when they're loaded, ride in her freight hold, and go into the far port's stores when she docks there. The fee is
# so many credits for each square of the hold the load takes and each measure of fuel the trip takes, times how badly
# it's wanted there (the same number that makes a thing dear where it's short), and it's paid into the purse on
# arrival. Late, it pays half; and only what's still in the hold is paid for. She can also buy any port's goods by
# the load for herself, out of the purse, and sell them wherever a port holds that good; those prices come from each
# good's item in world/items and from how much the port has.
#
# Passengers are offered at a port while the ship is docked there and you're ashore: a few people for another port on
# your chart. The fee goes into the purse when the ship gets there, and they eat at the sittings on the way.
#
# After changing this file, run "npm run check-story", and close and reopen the game.

the purse: 600 to begin with, 60 a day
a ration crate: 1 times the port's food price
a fuel cell: 0.15 times the port's fuel price
the slate: 1.5 times the price, up to 300 credits a port, standing falls 1 a day until it's paid
while she owes: the dock fills her stores to 33 percent
a distress call: answered in 3 days, at 2 times the price
freight: up to 3 offers a port, 4 credits a square for each measure of fuel the trip takes, 8 days to deliver
passengers: 4 to 10 people, 10 credits each
