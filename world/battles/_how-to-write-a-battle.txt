HOW TO WRITE A BATTLE
=====================

A battle is a fight fought by everyone on a deck at once, in the room, in real time with a pause, like any fight in
the room: the same rules, the same dice, the same numbers. What's different is that it isn't held to four on a side,
it's fought across the whole deck instead of one room, people come up the stairs as it goes, and there's no running
from it or giving up in it. Each battle is one file in this folder, named by its id: mutiny.txt is the battle
mutiny. A written scene starts one with a BATTLE line (world/scenes/_how-to-write-a-scene.txt says how), and npm run
check-story reads every battle and says what's wrong, with the file and the line.

Here is mutiny.txt, the battle that opens the game:

    # The Red Cloth
    place: meridian                              (the place it's fought at, by its id in world/places)
    deck: deck1                                  (the deck, by its id in the place's map: deck1 is Deck 1: Command)
    they make for: The Bridge                    (a room on that deck: whoever's against you heads for it, and
                                                  whoever's with you and already in it holds it)
    they give up: when 2 in 3 of them are out of it    (or never: out of it is down, or talked out of it)
    crew fight as: ship_hand                     (an enemy from world/enemies that the crew without a fight of their
                                                  own fight as, under their own names, with their own bodies)
    the rest take cover in: The Quiet Room       (a room on that deck: anyone there who doesn't fight goes there)
    when they give up: The rest of them throw their sidearms down.    (said when they give up)
    morale falls by: 5, and 2 for each of yours who went down, and 5 more if you went down
    the council's confidence falls by: 10, and 5 more if you went down
                                                 (what the battle costs aboard, when it's over: the
                                                  ship's morale and the council's confidence in you)

    Against you:
    - 6 mutineer_crew, up The Upper Forward Stair
    - 6 mutineer_crew, up The Upper Aft Stair, 12 seconds in: More of them come up the aft stair.

    With you:
    - everyone on the deck who can fight
    - tarrow, up The Upper Aft Stair, 60 seconds in: Lieutenant Tarrow comes up from below.
    - the guards, up The Upper Aft Stair, 60 seconds in
    - the ensigns, up The Upper Forward Stair, 60 seconds in

The lines at the top
--------------------

place, deck and they make for are needed. The others can be left out: without they give up, they fight until the
last of them is down; without crew fight as, a crew member with no fight of their own stays out of it and takes
cover; without the rest take cover in, whoever doesn't fight stays where they are; without morale falls by or the
council's confidence falls by, the battle costs nothing aboard. A line that starts with // is a note for whoever
writes, and draft: says who wrote it.

Against you
-----------

Each line is a group: how many, the enemy from world/enemies by its id, and where they come in: up a room (at its way
down to another deck, as if they'd come up the stairs) or in a room (anywhere in it). A group that comes later says
how many seconds in; one that comes at the start says nothing. The first line, or one of them, has to come at the
start. After a colon, what's said when they come.

People come in on the nearest six seconds: 12 seconds in is 12, 10 seconds in is 12 as well.

With you
--------

everyone on the deck who can fight is everyone on that deck when the battle starts who's on their feet: someone with
a fight in their file fights from it, and a crew member on the watch bill fights as the enemy crew fight as names.
The spare hands, and anyone else, take cover.

Any other line is someone who comes later, the same way as a group against you: a person by their id or name, like
tarrow, or a job on the watch bill, like the ensigns or the guards, which is everyone doing that job. Someone already
on the deck is already in it, and someone who's dead, down, in the brig or away doesn't come. A job nobody's doing
brings nobody, and its words aren't said.

How it ends
-----------

When everyone against you is down, talked out of it or has given up, it's won. If you go down, it goes on without
you, and you come to in the medbay once it's over: whoever's still against you when it's done gives up to the rest
of the crew. The scene that started the battle goes on from won, lost or talked.

Afterwards
----------

Whoever's beaten on your side lies where they fell, and the medics come for them, as after any fight. Whoever's
beaten on the other side lies there too, under the name the fight gave them (Mutineer #3), and the medics come for
them as well, after your own: they're carried to the medbay, treated with its medicine, and can bleed to death like
anyone, when their name goes up as an unnamed mutineer. Once they're on their feet they're walked to the brig. Those
who gave up, or were talked out of it, go straight there. The brig keeps them as a number, without names: the room
says how many, and so does the Command menu. Morale and the council's confidence fall as the file says, and everyone
who fought beside you remembers it.
