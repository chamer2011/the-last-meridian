HOW TO DRAW A ROOM IN TILED
===========================

The ship's rooms are drawn in Tiled, a free map editor, and the game reads them from there: one file to a room,
in world/maps/meridian/, named after the room, such as bridge.tmj. You lay a room out with tiles, put its people,
things and ways out in it, and save. The game draws the room its own way. The blocks you see in Tiled are only
stand-ins, so you can see what's where.


GETTING TILED
-------------

Tiled is free, from https://www.mapeditor.org. Its download page on itch.io asks for a price, and "No thanks, just
take me to the downloads" gets it for nothing. Install it the usual way.


OPENING THE SHIP
----------------

In Tiled, choose File > Open File or Project, and open almagest-explorers.tiled-project, here in world/maps. The
Project panel lists the ship's rooms under meridian. Double-click one to open it. Opening the project, rather than
a room on its own, also gives each kind of object the right boxes to fill in.

A room looks the way the game's isometric view sees it, from the same angle, built of plain blocks: grey walls,
floors, amber doors, teal consoles and machines, brown crates, and the stars outside a viewport.


ONE TILE, TWO OF THE GAME'S SQUARES
-----------------------------------

A tile is one square of floor the way the isometric view draws it. The game's own squares started as letters on a
screen, so they're half as wide as they are deep, and a tile covers two of them side by side. That's why some tiles
have something on one half only: a thin wall, a narrow door, the end of a console. Those keep the ship exactly the
way it was. Use them, or whole tiles, as you like.

People, things and ways out stand on one of the game's squares, so they can be on either half of a tile.

A room can be 39 tiles across and 18 down at most.


THE LAYERS
----------

The Layers panel has four, from the bottom up:

    Floor               the floor.
    Walls and things    walls, doors, consoles, crates, bunks, and anything else in the way. A tile here counts
                        instead of the floor under it, which only matters if you take the tile away.
    People and things   the people who stand here, the things you can look at and use (gold diamonds), the ways
                        out to other rooms (teal arrows), and where you stand when the story puts you here (the
                        flag).
    Words               words painted on the walls, and the names of parts of the room.

A layer you hide in Tiled, with its eye, is left out of the game too.


DRAWING
-------

Click the layer first, in the Layers panel. Then pick a tile in the Tilesets panel, from Stand-ins, choose the
Stamp Brush, and click or drag on the room. The Eraser takes tiles away, and Bucket Fill and Shape Fill fill an
area.

To see what a tile is, click it in the Tilesets panel: its "what" says, such as "wall (#)", or "wall (#) at the
back, floor (.) at the front" for a thin wall. Its mark is what it puts in the game's plan of the room, the same
characters the other places' rooms are drawn in, and its floor says which of those are floor. Leave both alone.


PEOPLE, THINGS AND WAYS OUT
---------------------------

These are on the People and things layer.

To move one, choose Select Objects and drag it. It stands on the square under its feet.

To add one, pick its marker in the Stand-ins tileset (the person, the gold diamond, the teal arrow or the flag),
choose Insert Tile, and click where it stands. Then fill in its properties, in the Properties panel:

    A person    Name is what the room calls them, such as Voss. person is who they are: the name of their file
                in world/characters, such as voss, or their full name. asks are what you can click to say to them
                first, one to a line, and asksOther the same for someone who isn't the captain. greeting is how
                they look when you come over, and side, left or right, is the side their name goes in the
                Walk-around view.
    A thing     Name is what it's called. look is what you read when you look at it, and use is what you do when
                you use it, which goes to the story. used is what happens, written out, with no AI, and
                usedOther the same for someone who isn't the captain. item is something you pick up from it.
    A way out   to is the room it leads to, by the name of its file, such as mess. Its Name is what it says on
                it, if that isn't the room's own name, and a way out named after the room it leads to needs no to
                at all. Put it in the room's outer wall. You come into the other room by its way back here. When
                there's more than one way back, via says which, by its mark.
    The flag    where you stand when the story puts you in this room. One to a room.

mark is the letter or number the game knows each of them by in its plan of the room. Leave it alone, or leave it
empty on a new one, and the game picks one.

Delete takes one away.


WORDS
-----

On the Words layer, choose Insert Text, click where the words start, and type them. In the Properties panel, set
Class to Writing for words painted on the walls, which cover what's under them so they can be read, or Label for
the name of a part of the room.


THE ROOM ITSELF
---------------

Map > Map Properties has:

    name                what the room's called.
    aliases             the other names the story might use for it, one to a line.
    desc                what you read about the room.
    floor               the marks that are floor. Leave it alone.
    floor color, walls color, scenery color, writing color
                        the room's colours in the game.


A NEW ROOM
----------

The easiest way is to start from one that's there: open it, choose File > Save As, and save it in the same folder
with the new room's name, in small letters with no spaces, such as cargo_bay.tmj. Change it, and give another room
a way out to it.

If you'd rather start from nothing, choose File > New > New Map: Isometric, a tile width of 128 and a height of
64. Add the stand-ins with Map > Add External Tileset, choosing tiles/stand-ins.tsj here, and add the four layers.
Save it in world/maps/meridian. Tiled's own kind of file, .tmx, is fine as well as .tmj.


SAVING
------

Ctrl+S, or File > Save. The game notices within a second or two, even while you're playing, and draws the room
again the next time the clock moves or you take a step. If a wall lands where you're standing, you're moved to the
nearest floor.

If something in a room is wrong, such as a person with no person, or a way out to a room that isn't there, the game
leaves that one thing out and draws the rest. npm run check-story lists what's wrong in plain words.


WHAT STAYS IN meridian.json
---------------------------

The ship's name, the room it starts in, and the deck plan: the map of the whole ship that opens from the button
beside the room. The deck plan is still drawn in characters, so it doesn't change when you redraw a room.

The other places still have their rooms in their own files here, drawn in characters. Any of them can be moved into
Tiled the same way, exactly as they are: node tools/to-tiled.mjs and the place's name, such as haven.
