HOW TO WRITE A PLACE'S LOGISTICS
================================

Nothing aboard comes from nothing. The ship's logistics say where every stock is kept, what machines turn one
thing into another, what each system draws from the generator, what each store wants kept in it and who
carries for it, and what gets restocked at a dock. It's one plain text file in this folder, world/logistics/<place>.txt, named after the
place (the file name of its map in world/maps). The Story board shows it under Logistics, as boxes and arrows,
and saving one there writes its file here.

    npm run check-story

checks these files too.

It's built in steps, each tried by the owner before the next. The game plays every part of the file now:
every store is a real thing in a room, full to start with, that the player can walk to and open beside their
pack; every machine takes from one store and puts into another on the clock; the generator burns fuel cells
and gives the power the systems draw, and lets them go in a written order when it hasn't enough; whenever a
store has fewer of something than it wants, whoever carries for it fetches some from the nearest store that
can spare it; the whole ship eats from the kettle at its sittings; everyone drinks from the water tank, which
the galley, the garden and the air plant take theirs out of too;
a store that's restocked is filled at a dock, out of the port's own stores and paid for from the ship's purse,
and in open space nothing comes; the engineers' repairs use up spare parts from the stores; and the Ship menu's
count of food, medicine and parts is the count of these stores, nothing else.

Then come the parts, each a heading with a colon and lines that start with - and a space.


STORES
------

A store is a thing in a room. Each line is the store's name, in, the room it's in, a colon, holds, what it
holds, and, if you like, another colon and how it looks:

    - pantry in The Galley: holds 12 ration crates, 12 produce: Shelves behind a wire door.

After what it holds, with a semicolon before each, a store can say what it wants kept in it, when that's
urgent, and who carries for it (see CARRYING):

    - pantry in The Galley: holds 12 ration crates, 12 produce; wants 4 of each; urgent under 2; carried by the deckhands, the cooks: Shelves behind a wire door.

The room is one from the place's map, by its name (The Galley, Engineering, Medbay). What it holds is a
number and an item from world/items each, by the item's name or its id, plural or not: ration crates,
fuel cells, parts crates, medicine crates, produce. That's what the store holds when the game starts, and
also the most it holds, so it starts full. A store of sittings (holds 400 sittings) is a count, not things
you can pick up: the kettle is one. So are the water tank (holds 800 measures of water) and the reserve tanks
(holds the reserve air): see WATER AND RESERVE AIR.

A freight hold is a store that says holds freight and nothing else:

    - freight hold in The Aft Holds: holds freight: Tie-down rings and netting.

It keeps nothing of the ship's own and starts empty. Whatever she carries for a fee, or buys by the load to
sell, rides in it as things, as far as its thirty squares go, and it's opened like any store. It wants
nothing and nobody carries for it; the ship's food, medicine and parts don't count what's in it.
world/trade.txt has the freight line.

If the room already has a thing with the store's name, that thing is the store, and it keeps its own look.
If it hasn't, the game puts one there, on a free square of the room, so nothing needs drawing to start.
Draw one in Tiled later if you'd rather, with the same name, and the game will use yours.

What's taken out and put back is kept in the saved game, and the Ship menu reads the same count.


MACHINES
--------

A machine takes from one store and puts into another, so often, on the clock. Each line is the machine's
name, then in and its room if the room isn't named like it (galley, garden), a colon, and what it does:

    - galley: cooks 1 produce or 1 ration crate from the pantry into 200 sittings in the soup kettle every hour, with power, a cook there and 1 water
    - garden: grows 1 produce into the garden bins every 2 hours, with power, someone tending it and 1 water
    - generator in Engineering: burns 1 fuel cell from the fuel rack every 8 hours, gives 100 power
    - air plant in Engineering: takes 1 water from the water tank every 3 hours, with power

The pieces, in any order after the verb:

    1 produce or 1 ration crate       what it uses each time, one of the choices, whichever the store has
    from the pantry                   the store it takes that from, which has to keep those items
    into 200 sittings in the soup kettle    what it makes and the store it goes into: sittings into a store of
                                      sittings, or an item into a store whose holds line keeps that item
    into the garden bins              the same, when the machine makes the one thing it names and uses nothing
    every hour / every 2 hours / every 30 minutes    how often it does that
    with power and a cook there       what it needs: power in its room, and someone there (any words after
                                      with that aren't power mean someone, and they're shown when nobody is;
                                      words that name a job on the watch bill, like a cook, mean someone
                                      doing that job, world/command says who)
    and 1 water                       with its needs: so many measures out of the place's water tank for every
                                      go, and it waits, saying the tank has no water, when there aren't enough
    gives 100 power                   a machine that gives power and makes nothing else is the generator: it
                                      starts with one of what it burns in it, takes the next from its store when
                                      that's spent, and stops, with nothing aboard powered, when there's none
    takes 1 water from the water tank    a machine that only takes water uses it up as it runs: it starts with
                                      one go's worth in it, takes the next when that's spent, and stops when the
                                      tank is empty. In the room of the place's air plant, it is the air plant

A machine is at work while its room has power and someone's there: the player standing in it, or anyone
whose day puts them there, awake. Its progress builds up to one go, and when there's room in the store it
puts into, and something to make it from, it goes. Otherwise it waits, and the Ship menu says why: no power,
waiting for a cook there, the soup kettle is full, the pantry has no produce or ration crates. Nothing
happens while the clock is paused.

A machine that makes sittings can also take any food: write it as one more choice, after the things it takes by
name, as the galley does (cooks 1 produce or 1 ration crate or any food from the pantry into 200 sittings in the
soup kettle every hour). The things named are taken first, in the order written. With none of them in the store, it
takes whatever else there is worth something at the kettle (an item's feeds line), up to one pot's worth, and makes
as many sittings as that was worth. What a thing named is worth is its own file's to say too: a ration crate that
feeds 150 makes 150 sittings, whatever the line's number, which is then only the size of the pot.

THE SITTINGS
------------

Everyone aboard eats from the stores of sittings (the kettle) at the sittings, as many a day each as
everyone aboard eats says, spread evenly through each sitting. Whoever finds the kettle empty goes without,
anyone you can see eating finds it empty instead, and at midnight the ship's morale feels it by the share
who went hungry, the way a food shortage does. The Ship menu shows the sittings, and what the kettle gave
today and yesterday.

WATER AND RESERVE AIR
---------------------

A place keeps its water in a store that holds measures of water, and its reserve air in a store that says
holds the reserve air:

    - water tank in The Pump Room: holds 800 measures of water: The ship's water, behind the pumps.
    - reserve tanks in Life Support: holds the reserve air: A rack of grey bottles along the hull.

Like the kettle they're counts, not things to pick up, and each starts full. A place has one of each at most.
The reserve tanks hold as much as the place's systems file gives it (reserve air: 5, in days, in
world/systems), so that number isn't written here: change it there.

Eight lines at the top say what the water does:

    everyone aboard drinks: 9 measures of water a day
    a tank of water fills: 100 measures
    with the pumps stopped: 4 times as much water goes
    water is rationed under: 200 measures
    a reserve tank gives: 24 hours of air
    a day of reserve air takes: 4 measures of water
    a breached room takes: 6 hours of reserve air
    a breached room fills in: 3 hours with no reserve air

everyone aboard drinks is for everyone the ship starts with, all together, in a day; with fewer aboard it's
less. It isn't all the water people use: it's what's lost for good, since the pumps bring the rest back.

Machines take water too (see MACHINES). One whose needs end with so much water takes that many measures out of
the tank for every go. One that only takes water uses it up as it runs, and in the room where the place's
systems have their air plant, that machine is the air plant: it makes the place's air from water and power.
When the tank is empty and what it had is used, it makes no air, exactly as if it had no power: the place
breathes its reserve air, and when that's gone, nothing (world/health/wounds.txt says what that does to
people). It uses nothing while it's switched off, broken or without power.

The pumps are the parts of the place's Water system (in world/systems: a part under a Water: heading, such as
- pumps in The Pump Room). While none of them works (broken, switched off, or with no power in its room),
nothing that's used comes back, and everything above takes with the pumps stopped times as much. With no
Water system written, the pumps never stop.

water is rationed under: when the tank has fewer measures than this and no dock is keeping it up, whoever
decides what the stores want (the what stores want line) puts water on ration at their next look. Everyone
drinks half, and whatever grows (a machine that needs water and makes things, not sittings) goes at half its
pace; the galley cooks as before. It comes off once the tank is back to half as much again, or at a dock that
sells water. With nobody in that post, or no such line, nobody rations.

The tank is filled at a dock by a Refills line (the water tank: water from the dock, every 6 hours, up to
full): whole tanks of water out of the port's own stock (the good the ports' files call water), each filling
a tank of water fills measures, paid for from the ship's purse at the item's own price, by how much the port
has. In open space nothing comes. A tank of water carried aboard can be poured in by hand at the water tank.

The reserve air is breathed while the air plant is stopped. It fills again while the air plant runs (reserve
air fills in, in world/systems/_defaults.json), and filling it takes water: a day of reserve air takes so
many measures. A full reserve tank from a port, fitted by hand at the reserve tanks, adds a reserve tank
gives hours. And when a breach is patched, the room is filled again out of the reserve tanks at once: a
breached room takes so many hours of their air. With less than that left in them, the air plant fills the
room alone, which takes a breached room fills in hours, and the room's air stays bad until then.

The Ship menu's Stores list has a Water line and an Air line, each with how long it lasts, and the Work page
lists both tanks with a word beside them when something's up.

CARRYING
--------

Nobody writes the routes. A store says what it wants kept in it, and the ship's work board does the rest:

    - fuel rack in Engineering: holds 6 fuel cells; wants 3; urgent under 2; carried by the deckhands, the techs, the spare hands
    - pantry in The Galley: holds 12 ration crates, 12 produce; wants 4 of each; carried by the deckhands, the cooks, the quartermaster, the spare hands
    - parts lockers in Stores: holds 6 parts crates; wants 2

wants is a number of one of the things the store holds (wants 4 ration crates, 2 produce), or a number alone
or with of each, for everything it holds. It can't be more than the store holds. Whenever the store has fewer
than it wants, counting what's already on its way, a job of carrying comes up. A store that wants nothing is
only ever taken from: the shuttle stores, the garden's bins.

Where it's fetched from isn't written: any other store that holds the same thing can supply it, as long as it
has more than it wants itself, and the nearest one is used. What a carrier has gone for is set aside for
them, so two people never go for the same crates, and as many go at once as the want needs. If nothing aboard
can be spared, nobody is sent, and the Ship menu says there's nothing aboard to bring.

urgent under is the smaller number at which the want jumps the queue: urgent jobs are given out before the
others, and one wakes a sleeper if nobody else is up. A want is also urgent, whatever its line says, when
whatever uses the thing up is asking: a machine with nothing left in the store to work from (the generator on
its last cell, the galley with a bare pantry), the medbay with someone waiting and no doses left, a repair
waiting for its spare.

carried by is who's asked, first to last: the people doing a job on the watch bill (the deckhands, the techs,
the cooks), whoever holds a post (the quartermaster), the spare hands, or someone by name (a crowd name from
the place's file in world/life, such as Tech Oona, or the name of someone the game knows). Everyone in the
first group is asked before anyone in the second. A store that wants something but names nobody is carried
for by the carried by line at the top of the file.

a trip carries, at the top, is how many of each thing one person carries at a time (2 ration crates, 2 fuel
cells, 4 produce). Anything it doesn't name goes one at a time. A carrier takes a trip's worth, or as many as
the store they're filling has room for, or as many as can be spared, whichever is least.

Whoever takes a job leaves what they were doing, walks to the store they fetch from, stands there a minute,
takes their load, walks it over, puts it in, and goes back to their day. One person has one job: the board
sends nobody who's already on another (see URGENCY for when someone leaves one job for another), nor anyone
taken by something outside it: answering an alarm, down or carrying the fallen, or walking with the captain.
The next of the carriers goes instead, and with nobody free the job waits. A carrier who's called away hands
the job back: whatever they'd loaded is set down, and the next free hand carries it on. A walk takes as long
as the way through the decks is. The Work page says what each store has against what it wants, who's carrying
for it and since when, or why nobody is.

An older file's Errands part (who carries what from which store to which) isn't read any more, and the
checker says so.

THE OFFICERS' ORDERS
--------------------

Two lines at the top say who runs the work board, since the captain doesn't:

    what comes first: the first officer, every hour
    what stores want: the quartermaster, every 4 hours

Each names a post from the place's chain of command (world/command), and how often whoever holds it looks at
how things stand: every hour, every 4 hours, every 30 minutes. Someone good at the post's own skill looks
sooner, and someone who isn't looks later (the command file's each point of skill line says by how much).
Someone new in the post looks at once.

what comes first: when its holder looks, they put one part of the ship first. The medbay, when people are
lying where they fell, or waiting with no medicine to give them. The engines, when the generator has stopped,
or is on its last cell, or its store is under its urgent mark. The galley, when the kettle is empty, the cooks
have nothing to cook, or people went hungry today. Repairs, when they're waiting for parts. It's whichever is in
the most trouble, and none of them when nothing's pressing. What a store wants belongs to the part that uses it:
medicine to the medbay, what the generator burns to the engines, what the galley cooks from to the galley, what
repairs take to repairs. Jobs in the part that's first go before the rest, after the urgent ones, which matters
when there are more jobs than free hands.

what stores want: when its holder looks, a store that ran low since their last look (its want was urgent) wants
a trip's worth more from then on, up to what it holds, and one that was raised and has been quiet for three
days is eased back a step, down to what its line says.

With nobody in the post, or its holder dead, locked up or laid up when their look comes, nobody decides: jobs
go as they come, and the stores want what they wanted. Leave a line out and nobody ever decides that. The Work
page says whose order stands, since when and why.

The player can take any job nobody's on, from the Work page (I'll do it): the crew leaves it alone for two
hours, or three quarters of an hour if it's urgent, and takes it back after that.

LEFT WAITING
------------

Two more lines at the top say when a job the board can't get done is brought to the player, and by whom:

    left waiting: 30 minutes if it's urgent, 3 hours if it isn't
    who asks: the chief medical officer for the medbay, the chief engineer for the engines and repairs, the quartermaster for the galley, the deck chief for the rest

A job is waiting when it's asking for a hand and nobody who does that work could come, even if they were woken
(they're laid up, called away, or on work that can't be left), or when it's asking for goods and there are none
aboard: a store that's short with nothing to bring, a repair or a breach with no parts crate. Someone who's only
asleep will see to it in the morning, so that isn't waiting; nor is the pantry short of produce, which the
garden is growing.

left waiting: how long that has to go on, for urgent work (a fire, a breach, a repair the ship can't do
without, and carrying that's urgent: a store under its urgent mark, or with whatever uses it standing idle) and
for everything else. Minutes, hours or days.

who asks: the post that brings it, for each part of the ship: the medbay, the engines, the galley, repairs, and
the rest. A job belongs to the part that uses what it's for, as for what comes first; a fire, a breach and a
repair belong to repairs. With nobody in the post, or its holder laid up, nobody brings that part's work.

Then whoever holds that post has something to ask, like any other request (world/scenes, REQUESTS in its
how-to). For a job with no hands, anyone who lives aboard is asked to do it themselves, and yes makes it theirs
on the Work page until it's due: request_work_hands.txt has the words. For a job with no goods, the captain is
asked to bring them aboard, and only for what the ship can buy (ration crates, medicine crates, parts crates):
request_work_goods.txt. Change what's said in those two files.

Where a request written by hand already asks about the same shortage (medicine for the medbay, a bare pantry,
something broken), that one asks and the board's keeps quiet; delete or turn off the one written by hand and the
board's takes over. The same job isn't brought twice within the time any request rests.

Both lines go together. Leave them out and nobody is ever brought anything, though the Work page still says why
each job is stuck.

URGENCY
-------

The Urgency part is the order the board's work comes in, the most urgent first, a kind of work to a line:

    Urgency:
    - carrying someone who's bleeding
    - a fire
    - a breach
    - carrying someone who's down
    - the alarm
    - urgent carrying
    - a repair the ship can't do without
    - treating someone
    - carrying the dead
    - carrying
    - a repair
    - mending in the workshop

Those are all the kinds there are, written just so. Move a line up or down and the ship chooses differently. A
kind the part leaves out comes after the ones it has, in the order above, and a place with no Urgency part
uses the order above.

The order does two things. Jobs are given out from the top: when there are more jobs than hands, the higher
line gets the hands. And someone who's already at work leaves it only for a job two lines higher or more: a
tech mending a speaker (a repair) leaves it to fetch the generator's fuel (urgent carrying, five lines up), but
not for a crate the pantry merely wants (carrying, one line up). What they leave waits for the next free hand: a
load is set down where they were, and a repair is simply still broken, with an hour's work for whoever takes it
up. Within one line, the part of the ship the first officer has put first goes before the rest.

urgent carrying is carrying for a store that's under its urgent mark, or that a machine, the medbay or a
repair is waiting on (see CARRYING); carrying is the rest. a repair the ship can't do without is one to
whatever carries her power (the junction boxes) or to the air plant; a repair is anything else that's broken.
The top seven kinds, as the list first has them, wake someone when nobody who could do the job is up; the rest
wait for the morning. (Whether a kind wakes people goes with the kind, not with where its line is.)

The carrying and the repairs are the board's own. The medbay and the workshop keep their own records of who's
carrying whom, who's treating whom and what's on the bench (world/health), and the board reads those as jobs
like its own. Who answers a call for someone down is the board's choice: the health file's who answers a call
line is still the order they're asked in, and the first of them who's free, or who can leave lesser work for
it, goes; someone bleeding comes before someone who's only down (among the place's own people: someone with no
day there, such as a beaten mutineer, is carried in their turn). A patient who's waiting takes a medic off
lesser work in the same way, and a medic or a mender who's on other work for the board is left to it. Work
left at the workshop's bench is set down with what was left of it kept, and taken up again where it was.

A fire and a breach are the board's own jobs too: an engineer (the place's systems file says who they are) is
sent to each the moment it starts, walks there, and is on it until the fire's out or the hull's patched.
world/emergencies.txt still says how long each takes, counted from when it started: a fire can't be turned
sooner, and isn't fought at all while nobody's got there. The guards answering an alarm are on a job as well,
which nothing takes them off, and carry nothing meanwhile.

LOADS
-----

The loads say what each system draws from the generator, and the order they're let go in when the generator
hasn't enough for them all:

    - air plant 20, engines 30 while under way, doors 6, comms 6, navigation 6, cameras 4, lights 1 a room, computers 2 each
    - when short, drop: cameras, comms, lights, doors, navigation, air

A load is a system (air plant or air, engines, doors, comms, navigation, cameras, lights, alarm, docking) or
computers, and a number: what it draws while it has any part switched on and whole. With a room after it, the
number is for each room with such a part switched on (the lights); with each, for each such part (or each
computer). With while under way, it only draws during a jump (the engines). A system that isn't written draws
nothing.

When what's drawn is more than the generator gives, the systems in the drop line are let go, first to last,
until what's left fits; a system let go is dead, as if its room's power were cut: the rooms go dark, the air
stops, the cameras see nothing, and the Ship menu's Power section, the reactor gauges' readouts and any
computer's Power screen say so. A system not in the drop line is never let go. When the generator's stopped
altogether, nothing aboard has power.

REFILLS
-------

A refill tops a store up from outside. Each line is the store, a colon, what comes into it, where from, how
often, and how far it's filled:

    - the shuttle stores: ration crates and fuel cells from the dock, every 6 hours, up to full

From the dock is how the ship lives. While she's docked at a port that sells what's named (a port that sells
food sells ration crates, and one that sells fuel sells fuel cells), every so many hours each item is brought
up to what the store's holds line keeps, or up to the number written after up to. They're real crates and
cells out of the port's own stores, so a port that's down to six sells six, and they're paid for out of the
ship's purse at the port's price that day. world/trade.txt has the prices, and what happens when the purse is
empty. In open space nothing comes.

A line can still say from nothing (the words for now are allowed after it). Then the store is topped up from
nowhere, wherever the ship is, on the hour counted from midnight (every 6 hours is at 00:00, 06:00, 12:00 and
18:00). That's how every game went before the ports held real amounts, and it's there for trying things out.

The water tank is filled the same way, by the tank (see WATER AND RESERVE AIR):

    - the water tank: water from the dock, every 6 hours, up to full

The store has to keep every item named. What came aboard is written in the manifest, which you read by
looking at the store (the shuttle stores carry it on a clip) or on any of the ship's computers that lists the
manifest log, and the Ship menu's Refills lines say the last one.

REPAIRS
-------

The line at the top that starts with a repair takes says what the engineers use up each time they fix
something that's been damaged: so many of an item, from which stores, first to last, and for which kinds of
damage:

    a repair takes: 1 parts crate, from the parts lockers or the shuttle stores, for what's broken, cut, crippled or forced

The engineers are the ones the place's systems file names (world/systems). In the last hour before something's
due to be fixed, the work board gives it to the first of them who's free, in the order that file has them (the
chief engineer, then the techs): they spend the first half of the hour fetching what it takes from the first
of those stores with enough in it, and the second half at the part, and it comes out of the store when the
part's fixed. One engineer mends one thing at a time. With none of them free the repair waits, and its hour
starts when someone takes it; an ordinary repair wakes nobody, and one the ship can't do without does (see
URGENCY). With no spare in any of the stores, the repair waits: the story says so once, the part's look says
what it's waiting for, and the Work page and the Ship menu's Repairs lines list it. About an hour after some
comes aboard, it's fixed. The Work page lists every repair: who's on it, when the engineers will start, or
why nobody has. The player can take one from there (I'll do it), and then mends it by hand.

The kinds of damage are what each kind of part's break line does to it (cut, jammed, sealed, covered, crippled,
scrambled, broken), and forced, for a door. A kind left out of the line is fixed with nothing, the way a rag
comes off a camera's lens or a seal off a vent. Leave out from and every store that keeps the item is tried;
leave out for what's and every kind of damage takes it; write a repair takes: nothing, or leave the line out,
and repairs take nothing at all. Fixing something yourself takes nothing, and neither does a story's * fix.

SALVAGE
-------

The line at the top that starts with salvage makes says what the player can make at the bench from the salvage
they've found (step 6 of the loot plan): so many of an item, from how much salvage, the room it's made in, how
long it takes, and the stores it goes into, first to last:

    salvage makes: 1 parts crate from 10 salvage, in The Repair Bay, in 2 hours, into the parts lockers

Each item's own salvage line says what a piece counts for (world/items: a circuit board 2, a reactor seal ring 15,
scrap 1). In that room, You can offers it, greyed with the reason while the player hasn't enough or is too tired.
It uses up the least valuable salvage first, takes the hours less a tenth for each point of the player's
Engineering (never under half), practises Engineering, and puts what's made into the first of the stores with
room, or into the player's pack; leave out into and it always goes into the pack. What's made into a store counts
as brought aboard, for anyone who's asked for crates. Write salvage makes: nothing, or leave the line out, and
nothing is made.

THE COUNT ON THE SHIP MENU
--------------------------

The Ship menu's Stores (food, medicine, parts) are these stores and nothing else: food is every ration crate
and basket of produce aboard, counted as crates; medicine the medicine crates; parts the parts crates. The
kettle feeds everyone through the day; the medbay and the engineers take a crate at midnight each time they've
used one up; a day's use and the days left are worked out from that. Buying at a dock puts crates into the
stores that keep them (food into the shuttle stores, then the pantry) and selling takes them out, both through
the ship's purse, and a story that gives or takes food counts one of its units as ten crates, about a day's
eating. Fuel for jumps stays in the tank, which has nothing to do with the fuel cells the generator burns: a
cell can't be poured into the tank, and jump fuel can't run the generator.
