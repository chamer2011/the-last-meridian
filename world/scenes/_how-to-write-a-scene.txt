HOW TO WRITE A SCENE
====================

A scene is a plain text file in this folder, like opening_captain.txt. Open it in Notepad, change
it, save it, and the game uses the new version the next time the story reaches it. There's no need
to restart. To find mistakes, run this in the game's folder:

    npm run check-story

It lists every problem with the file and the line it's on. Nothing is changed by it. The tests
(npm test) check the scenes too.

Files whose names start with _ are notes like this one, and aren't scenes.


WHAT THE FILE'S NAME MEANS
--------------------------

opening_captain.txt    How the story opens for the captain. For another life, use its id from
                       starts.json: opening_engine_hand.txt, opening_docker.txt, and so on.
opening.txt            The opening for any life that has none of its own.
thread_<story>.txt     Plays when a story from the Leads menu is taken up, for example
                       thread_crew_naomi_under_crew.txt. The ids are the file names in world/threads.
arrive_<place>.txt     Plays when the player arrives at that place, by jump, passage or shuttle, when
                       they press Continue the story. The ids are the file names in world/places.
talk_<person>.txt      What that person says in a talk. See CONVERSATIONS below.
request_<kind>.txt     Something someone aboard asks the player when something goes wrong, like
                       request_medicine.txt. See REQUESTS below.


THE SHAPE OF A SCENE
--------------------

    # The title
    start: bridge

    == bridge
    Paragraphs of what the player reads. Lines run together until a blank line, which
    starts a new paragraph.

    @Commander Ilya Voss: "Someone speaking gets a line like this, with their name after @."

    => A line starting with => is a consequence, shown boxed, for something that will matter later.

    > A choice -> the_part_it_leads_to
    > Another choice -> somewhere_else

    == somewhere_else
    More text.
    END

Each part starts with == and a name made of letters, numbers and underscores. The first part is
where the scene starts, unless a start: line says otherwise. A part either has choices, or ends the
scene with END, or opens a check or a fight (see below), or has a rule that sends the story on.
Lines starting with // are comments, and the game ignores them.

Speech should be written exactly like the example, with the name after @ and the words in quotes.
That's how the words go in a window over that person's head when they're in the room. The game
knows the people in world/characters by their names and the names people call them.


WHO A PART WAITS FOR
--------------------

A part waits for whoever speaks last in it, since that's who the player is answering. The story
goes on only when the player is in the same room as them: until then, the quest tracker in the
corner of the room says who to talk to and where they are, and the part plays when the player
walks over and clicks them. While whoever the next part needs is in the room, the scene goes
straight on. If the player walks out in the middle, the quest is on hold until they're back.

    WITH: Commander Ilya Voss     (this part waits for her, whoever speaks last)
    WITH: nobody                  (this part plays wherever the player is)

A story's first part plays at once, and whoever speaks in it comes over. So does someone who
lives at another place, or is behind a door the story hasn't opened yet, since they can't be
walked to. A voice over the comms isn't waited for, and nor is anyone with no file in
world/characters. A part with a CHECK or a FIGHT plays wherever the player is. If someone a part
is waiting for has died, the quest fails, and the story can't go on.


CHOICES
-------

    > [+Voss] Ask her what she'd do -> voss_answer
    > [persuasion:moderate:voss] Ask what she isn't telling you -> voss_pressed_pass | voss_pressed_fail
    > [reason] Tell the fleet the truth -> truth
    > [! costs 200 credits] Pay him -> paid ? credits >= 200

Things in square brackets before the words are tags, which the player sees:

    [persuasion:moderate]        A skill check. The skill's id and the difficulty: easy, moderate,
                                 hard, very_hard or nearly_impossible. Add a person's id after a
                                 second colon when they're the one being persuaded, deceived or led,
                                 and how they feel about the player changes the odds. A choice with
                                 a check leads two ways: the part if it passes | the part if it fails.
                                 For hacking, the difficulty says what kind of computer it is, and
                                 the window shows it: easy is a door panel, moderate a crew terminal,
                                 hard a ship's main system (two passwords), very_hard a Ministry or
                                 Navy server, nearly_impossible the Beacon or the Choir (three).
                                 A hack has no roll: getting in is the pass, the trace completing
                                 or disconnecting is the fail. A completed trace on a ship's main
                                 system or worse leaves a fact on the record, traced_on_<class>,
                                 which a condition can ask about: ? flag traced_on_ships_main_system.
                                 A third word names the computer the hack gets into when it passes,
                                 by its file in world/computers: [hacking:moderate:haven_berth_board].
                                 Without one, it lands on this place's computer of that class, or one
                                 the game makes up. Getting in opens the computer's own system.
    [+Voss] or [-Tarrow]         Someone who would clearly approve or disapprove.
    [reason]                     The belief the choice expresses: order, freedom, community, self,
                                 mercy, iron, reason, faith, purity or ascension.
    [! anything]                 A warning, such as a cost or a risk.

A choice can end with ? and a condition, and then it's only offered when the condition holds.
Conditions are explained below.


WHAT A PART CHANGES
-------------------

Lines starting with * happen when the part begins, and the player sees each as a game message:

    * journal: Words to write in the journal.
    * xp 10: The reason
    * credits -20: The reason               (a minus sign charges the player)
    * item +medkit                          (gives an item from world/items.json or src/data.js)
    * item -data_chip                       (takes one away)
    * quest the_beacon: The new objective   (changes a quest the player already has)
    * quest the_beacon done: How it ended   (or failed)
    * new quest find_the_scholar: The Title | What it's about | What to do first
    * contact Ilya Voss friendly: What happened between you     (hostile, wary, neutral, friendly or ally)
    * belief reason 1: The reason           (1 for a telling choice, 2 for a major one, 3 for a defining moment)
    * faction fleet 2: The reason           (a minus sign lowers it)
    * stress 5: The reason                  (a minus sign relieves it)
    * health -4: The reason                 (a minus sign hurts, and it can't kill)
    * time 2 hours: The reason              (add work or rest after hours if that's what it was; 72 at most in one part)
    * flag told_the_fleet                   (remembers something, for conditions later, in any scene)
    * flag kessara_closed: Kessara is closed to the fleet     (with words, the player reads it on the record,
                                            in the Quests menu)
    * unflag told_the_fleet
    * ending sign_with_quill: How it ended   (ends this scene's story by one of the endings named in its file,
                                            world/stories: the quest is done with that line, and the ending's on
                                            the record. Put failed before the colon if the player lost it)
    * fuel 6: The reason                    (the Meridian's fuel, which jumps use; a minus sign burns it)
    * act 2                                 (the main story moves on to that act)
    * door sublevels                        (opens the door to that room, by the room's id in the map file,
                                            in the place the player is at)
    * password haven_berth_board ORBIT      (the player learns that computer's password, and gets in with
                                            no hack; the computers are files in world/computers)
    * computer haven_berth_board            (sits the player down at that computer, as if they'd got in)
    * power off in The Docks                (the place's systems: power, lights, air, cameras, alarm, comms,
    * alarm ringing in The Docks             engines, navigation, docking, door locked or unlocked, fix and
    * door locked The Hook                   break. world/systems/_how-to-write-systems.txt has them all, and
    * fix everything                         what each one does)
    * appoint quartermaster tobin: The reason   (the ship's chain of command: someone given a post, by its id,
                                                which whoever chooses for it gives them; not the captain's chair)
    * relieve chief_engineer: The reason        (whoever holds it relieved, and the post given to the best of those
                                                it's picked from, as at a review)
    * assign Yusuf cook: The reason             (someone moved on the watch bill: a job by its id, or spare)
    * loyalty marrow -10: The reason            (their loyalty to the captain, a memory that fades like the rest)
    * remember marrow tarrow -15: The reason    (Marrow remembering this of Tarrow, for or against them; you for the
                                                player)
    * mutiny give ground: The reason      (the ship's chain of command, for the scenes it plays: give ground,
                                          calmed, arrest, crushed, step down, refused, taken, released, join,
                                          stay out or warn; world/command/_how-to-write-command.txt says what each does)
    * raw add_lore {"term": "The Rim", "kind": "place", "text": "What the player would know."}

The last one does any of the game's actions, with the fields from src/tools.js. The same rules
hold: there are limits on credits and XP in one turn, and a quest has to exist before it can
change.


CONDITIONS
----------

A condition goes after ? on a choice, or starts a rule line. Put "not" in front to turn one round,
and join several with "and":

    has data_chip                    The player carries that item.
    credits >= 20                    Also <=, >, <, = and !=, for credits, stress, health (as a
                                     percentage), day, and level.
    skill persuasion >= 3            A skill's rank. Also stat presence >= 4, faction fleet >= 5,
                                     and belief reason >= 2.
    flag told_the_fleet              A flag set with * flag.
    ended main_two_weeks_of_fuel     That story is over, any way it went.
    ended main_two_weeks_of_fuel raid_depot    It ended that way. The names are in its file, world/stories.
    seen voss_answer                 The player has been through that part in this scene.
    quest the_beacon active          Also done, failed, or none if they haven't got it.
    met Voss                         They're in the player's contacts.
    likes Voss                       Friendly or better. dislikes Voss is wary or worse.
    captain                          The player is the captain.
    life engine_hand                 The player started as that life. The ids are in starts.json.
    at haven                         The player is at that place.
    power off here                   The place's systems: power, lights, air (good, thin, bad, on, off),
    alarm ringing at haven           cameras, alarm (ringing, quiet, on, off), door locked and a room,
    door locked The Hook             comms, engines, navigation, docking, and system and a name. Add
                                     here, or in and a room, and at and a place. world/systems has the rest.
    act < 2                          The main story's act, with the same comparisons as credits.
    fuel < 3                         The Meridian's jump fuel, with the same comparisons.
    stock medicine_crate < 2         How many of an item the Meridian's stores hold, all of them together,
                                     by the item's id. Add in and a store for one of them: stock
                                     ration_crate < 1 in the pantry. The stores are in world/logistics.
    chance 30                        True 30 times in 100, at random.
    holds quartermaster odessa       Who holds a post aboard the Meridian: the post by its id, then someone by
                                     their id or name, you, or nobody.
    job Cook Imre cook               What someone does on the watch bill: a job by its id, or spare.
    loyalty marrow < 40              Someone's loyalty to the captain, from 0 to 100, with the same comparisons
                                     as credits.
    mutiny brewing                   A mutiny's brewing aboard the Meridian. Also mutiny calm, grumbling,
                                     refusing orders and delegation (how far it's got), taken (the officers
                                     relieved the player of command, and they're crew now), can arrest (the
                                     chief of security would lock up whoever's leading it) and offers the chair
                                     (the officers would have the player back as captain).

A condition with a mistake in it is never true, and check-story points it out.


STAND-INS
---------

A scene the game starts by itself can leave a person for the game to fill in, in curly brackets:

    {ringleader}     whoever leads the officers against the captain, or led them when they took the ship
    {captain}        whoever has the chair: "you" in the words when it's the player
    {quartermaster}  whoever holds that post now, and the same for every post by its id: {first_officer},
                     {chief_engineer}, {chief_of_security}, {chief_medical_officer}, {deck_chief},
                     {comms_officer}, {helm_officer}, {sensor_officer}, {speaker}

In what's said and read it's their name, so @{ringleader}: "Captain." is them speaking; in a FIGHT, a WITH
line and a check's audience it's their id, so FIGHT {ringleader} fights them. Nobody standing in is "someone" in
the words. check-story reads a scene as if Kara Tarrow led a mutiny against Captain Reddick, and as if everyone held
the post they begin the game in, and says so if a word in curly brackets isn't one of these. A conversation can use
them too. command_delegation.txt, command_conspiracy.txt and command_brig.txt use them, and so does Hask's question
about the stores in talk_tobin.txt.


RULES: GOING SOMEWHERE ELSE
---------------------------

A line starting with ? and a condition, then an arrow, sends the story to another part instead of
showing this one, when the condition holds. A plain -> is where to go otherwise. They're checked
in order, before the text:

    == plot
    ? flag told_the_fleet -> plot_after_truth
    ? credits < 10 -> plot_broke

    @Ensign Pell Asante: "Plotted, sir."
    END

A part that only has rules, and a final -> part, is a fork in the road with no text of its own.

The rules are read again when the player presses Continue the story while they're in that part,
so a part can wait for something. A part with ? at kessara -> parlor and text telling the player
to set course from the Star map sends the story on to the parlor once the Meridian has jumped there
and they press Continue. The part's choices stay on offer meanwhile, for anything else they'd
rather do first.


A CHECK THE STORY DEMANDS
-------------------------

When the story itself calls for a check, rather than a choice:

    == hatch
    The hatch is locked, and there's no other way.
    CHECK engineering:moderate
      reason: Open the hatch
      pass -> open
      fail -> stuck

The indented lines belong to the CHECK. reason is what the player reads as the window opens.


A FIGHT
-------

    == ambush
    Three of them step out from behind the coolant tanks.
    FIGHT dock_thug
      lead_in: Nobody says anything. They don't need to.
      enemy_followers: dock_thug, drift_raider
      allies: voss
      why: paid
      reason: Somebody's paying them to break your legs.
      won -> won
      lost -> lost
      fled -> fled
      gaveUp -> gave_up
      talked -> talked_them_down

The enemy ids are the file names in world/enemies, and people are the file names in
world/characters. won and lost are needed, and the others are only if you want them: otherwise
running away or giving up counts as lost, and talking them down counts as won. why is why they're
fighting, which decides how hard they are to talk down: desperate, afraid, paid, greed, orders,
guarding, cause, belief, grudge or honor. The first line of text in a part with a FIGHT isn't
needed: the lead_in is what the player reads as it starts.


CONVERSATIONS
-------------

A file called talk_<id>.txt is what that person says when the player walks up to them, with the id
from world/characters: talk_voss.txt, talk_marrow.txt. It's written like a scene, and plays in the
talk window. The person speaks first, from the first part, and each choice is a thing the player can
say. A part with no choices is where the conversation runs out, and the player says goodbye.

    == start
    ? flag knows_about_the_list -> start_list
    @Commander Ilya Voss: "Captain. Go on."
    > What's our real situation? -> situation ? not seen situation
    > That's all for now -> done

    == situation
    @Commander Ilya Voss: "Fuel for two weeks."
    > That's all for now -> done

    == done
    @Commander Ilya Voss: "Captain."

In a conversation, "seen" means said in this conversation, so ? not seen situation hides a
question once it's been asked. Flags set anywhere last, so a conversation can know what the
story did. Effects (* lines) work, except time, travel and set_scene, because the clock stops
in a talk. A conversation can't open a CHECK or a FIGHT: put those in a scene.

Use ? not captain -> other at the start when the person would speak differently to someone who
isn't the captain. Anyone without a file of their own gets stock lines, made from who they are and
what they're doing, from the templates at the end of world/rooms.json.


REQUESTS
--------

A file called request_<kind>.txt is a request: when something goes wrong aboard, someone it
matters to has something to ask the player. While they do, there's an amber ! on their tag in
the room, and on their line in What's here, and the story says so once. When the player talks to
them, they ask first; once the player has answered, their own conversation goes on as usual.
Saying yes puts it in the player's quests, under Requests, with what's wanted and the day it's
due. It ends one of three ways, with that person's line in the story:

    done      the player brought what was needed
    gone      it was put right some other way first, so it isn't needed (no harm done)
    failed    the day came and it still wasn't done

On top, before the first part, five lines say when it comes up and what puts it right:

    # Medicine for the medbay
    when: captain and stock medicine_crate < 2
    asks: the chief medical officer
    needs: medicine crates aboard +2
    by: 5 days
    gone when: stock medicine_crate >= 4

when:       a condition, as in a scene. While it holds, they ask. If it stops holding before the
            player has said yes, they don't ask any more.
asks:       who asks: a post (the chief medical officer: whoever holds it now), a job (a cook: one
            of the cooks at work), or someone by name. Add ", in The Galley" to say where to find
            them, if it isn't where their post or job is.
needs:      what's wanted, where, and how many, with a + before the number:
                medicine crates aboard +2         bought for the ship from the Ship menu
                ration crates in the pantry +2    carried into that store by hand, or bought into it
                fuel in the tank +3               bought for the ship
            Only what the player brings counts. Taking something back out of a store takes it off.
by:         how long they give, once the player says yes: 5 days, or 12 hours.
gone when:  a condition that means it's been put right without the player. If it holds while the
            player hasn't brought anything yet, the request is gone; if they'd brought some, it's done.

Then the parts, in the talk window's way (see CONVERSATIONS), with {asker} for whoever's asking:

    == ask          what they ask. One of its choices goes to yes.
    == yes          what they say to a yes. The player takes it on.
    == later        what they say to "not now". They ask again a day later. (Needed only if a
                    choice goes there.)
    == no           what they say to a no. They don't ask again for a week.
    == done         what they say once it's done, with what the player gets: * xp 15: The reason,
                    * remember {asker} you 10: what they'll remember.
    == failed       what they say if the day comes first.
    == gone         what they say if it was put right without the player.

A part with no choices hands the talk back to their own conversation. Done, failed and gone are
said in the story, not in a talk, so their * lines can't move the clock or the player.

At most three requests wait at once, and someone new asks once a day at most. Each person asks one
thing at a time. A request that's over rests for a while before it can come up again: two days if
it was done or gone, four if it failed, a week after a no. Requests only come to someone who lives
aboard the Meridian, captain or crew, and a when: line can say more, like captain for anything
only the captain can buy.


THINGS AND DOORS
----------------

These are in the map files, world/maps/<place>.json, not here. A thing's used is what happens
when the player uses it (usedOther for someone who isn't the captain), and a thing
with an item is picked up. A closed door's ask is the rule when the player asks at it:

    "ask": { "check": "persuasion:moderate", "reason": "Talk your way past", "pass": "...", "fail": "..." }
    "ask": { "if": "flag quill_invited", "opens": "...", "else": "..." }
    "ask": { "opens": "...", "flag": "crossed_the_picket" }
    "ask": { "answer": "Not yet." }

if can be any condition from above. A door with if and check tries the condition first, then
the check. flag is remembered when the door opens.


GAME MESSAGES
-------------

    NOTE: [STORY] A message shown with the part, in the colored lines, not in the story's own voice.


NOTES FOR WHOEVER WRITES
------------------------

    AI: Sable won't go below forty, and a threat makes her stop talking.

An AI: line is a note in the file, and nothing more: the player never sees it, and the game does
nothing with it. They were written when the game had an AI game master, which read them to stay
true to the part. That's gone, so keep them, change them or delete them as you like.


WHAT THE PLAYER CAN TYPE
------------------------

The player picks from the choices, or types part of one. Typing is forgiving: "say nothing" is
enough for "Say nothing, and listen to it". Anything else is refused in plain words, and nothing
changes. So every part the player can stay in needs choices, and the choices have to cover every
way through.
