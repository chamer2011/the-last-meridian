HOW TO WRITE A PLACE'S CHAIN OF COMMAND
=======================================

Who holds which post, who chooses them, and what each post answers for. It's one plain text file in this folder,
world/command/<place>.txt, named after the place (the file name of its map in world/maps). Only the Meridian has
one so far.

    npm run check-story

checks these files too.

It's built in steps, each tried by the owner before the next. The game plays the posts and who holds them, each
post's duty, skill in the work, the watch bill, and the officers' reviews: the Command menu shows each post as a
card and each job with who's on it, everything in the other files that names someone for a post or a job (the
council's rations decisions in world/ship.json, the guards and engineers in world/systems, the machines and the
carriers of the errands in world/logistics, the ship's own lines in world/ship.json, and the captain's chair) asks
this file who that is, everyone on the watch bill lives their job's day, whoever does badly in a post is warned,
relieved and replaced, and when the officers' loyalty and the ship's morale run low, a mutiny builds.


THE FILE
--------

    # CFS Meridian
    draft: the AI's, not yet edited by the owner      (the game doesn't read this: it's for whoever edits)
    each point of skill: 8% quicker or slower        (how much each point of a worker's skill either side of 5
                                                     speeds the work or slows it: see SKILL)
    reviews every: 7 days                            (how often the officers review their people: see RISING AND
                                                     FALLING)
    a bad stretch: duty under 60 for 5 days          (when someone's warned, and then relieved at a review)
    the captain's choices stand: 10 days             (how long someone the captain put in a post, or kept on in
                                                     one, isn't reviewed)
    memories fade: by half every 30 days             (how fast people forget what was done to them)
    everyone else: ambition 2, temper 2, loyalty 60  (the numbers of whoever has none in their own file)
    mutiny brews: morale under 30, loyalty under 35  (when a mutiny brews: see LOYALTY AND MUTINY)
    the mutiny builds: grumbling after 1 day, orders refused after 4, a delegation after 7, the mutiny after 10
                                                     (all on one line: after how many days of brewing each stage
                                                     comes)
    the council's confidence: 60 to begin with       (the council's confidence in the captain when a game begins)
    the brig: The Brig, for 7 days                   (the room whoever's arrested is kept in, and for how long)

    Posts:
    - captain on The Bridge: chosen by the council; skills leadership, tactics
    - first officer on The Bridge: chosen by the captain; keeps the watch bill; skills leadership, composure
    - quartermaster in The Long Mess: chosen by the captain; heads cooks, mess hands, gardeners; picked from cooks,
      mess hands, gardeners, the deck chief; skills commerce            (all on one line in the file)

    Jobs:
    - cook in The Galley: 2 wanted; skills survival

    Who holds what:
    - captain: the chair
    - first officer: voss
    - quartermaster: odessa

    Who does which job:
    - cook: Cook Imre, Cook Dessa
    - guard: nobody
    - spare hands: Yusuf, Salome

The first line is the place's name. The lines after it, before the first part, are the numbers at the top. Then
come the parts, each a heading with a colon and lines that start with - and a space.


POSTS
-----

Each line is the post's name, in or on, the room it's kept in, a colon, and then what's said about it, each part
after a semicolon:

    chosen by the captain          who chooses whoever holds it: another post, or the council, or the
                                   civilians. Every post has one. Nobody chooses themselves, and following who
                                   chooses whom has to end at the council or the civilians.
    keeps the watch bill           the post that gives everyone their job, covers for anyone lost, and speaks for
                                   the captain in the reviews
    keeps the brig                 the post that arrests whoever's leading a mutiny, if they're loyal enough
    heads cooks, mess hands        the jobs it answers for, by their names in the Jobs part
    picked from cooks, the deck    who a new holder is picked from: jobs by their names in the Jobs part, and
    chief                          other posts. Without it, the jobs it heads. Never the post itself, nor the chair
    skills commerce, persuasion    the skills that count for it: the Command menu shows the holder's numbers
                                   in them, from their file in world/characters
    duty: sittings missed          what its holder is marked on each day: see DUTY

The room is one of the place's rooms, by its name in the maps. The Command menu groups the posts by who chooses
them, in the file's order.


JOBS
----

Each line is the job, in or on, the room it's done in, a colon, how many it wants, and its skills:

    - cook in The Galley: 2 wanted; skills survival

How many it wants is how many the watch bill counts it short by, when fewer are doing it. The Command menu shows the
job's room and the skills are kept for a later step.


WHO HOLDS WHAT
--------------

Each line is a post, a colon, and who holds it when a game begins: someone with a day at the place in
world/life, by their id (odessa), their name (Odessa Brandt, or Brandt) or their crowd name (Tech Sami). One post
each. The captain's post can be the chair: the player, if they started as the captain, and otherwise whoever the
game gave the chair, from the chair's list in world/ship.json. A post that nobody holds is a problem the checker
names.

A game keeps who holds what in its save from the moment it begins, so a change here reaches new games, and the
games under way keep theirs.


WHO DOES WHICH JOB
------------------

Each line is a job, a colon, and who does it when a game begins: people with a day at the place in world/life,
usually by their crowd name, or nobody. One job each, and nobody who holds a post. The line for spare hands names
the people the first officer can put to work where a job's short. Anyone not written here isn't on the watch bill
(the sick on their cots, say).

Each job's day is the day in world/life of the first person who starts in it. Someone moved to another job lives
that job's day, at their own shift, so they walk to its room and do what it does there; someone sent to the spare
hands lives the spare hands' day. A job nobody starts in gets a plain day: on duty in its room from six, two meals at
the benches, and a bunk at ten.

If the player started as crew aboard (the engine hand, the orderly, the marine), they're on the watch bill too, in
the job done in the room their own work is in, and a move there moves the shift they work.

The captain moves people from the Command menu: a click on someone, then Move here on the job. The first officer
(the post that keeps the watch bill) covers for anyone lost from their job, dead or being patched up after a
beating, with a spare hand, and sends the spare hand back once they're back, saying so in the story both times.

A game keeps its watch bill in its save from the moment it begins.


DUTY
----

At each midnight the holder of every post is marked out of 100 on each of its duty's measures for the day that's
ended, and their duty for the day is the average. The Command menu shows the average of their last week, and the
last day's marks. Someone new in a post starts with a clean record. The measures are:

    morale                  the ship's morale at midnight
    shortages               25 off for each of food, fuel, medicine and parts that's run out
    deaths aboard           5 off for each person who died aboard that day
    stations staffed        how much of what the watch bill wants is being done
    sittings missed         the share of the day's sittings that were served
    stores run dry          34 off for each of food, medicine and parts that's run out
    power short             about 8 off for each hour the generator was short of what's drawn
    repairs waiting         30 off for each repair waiting for parts at midnight
    hull worn               the hull, out of its best
    alarms                  30 off for each time the alarm rang
    tampering seen          30 off for each time a camera saw tampering
    thefts                  25 off for each pocket picked aboard
    medicine out            nothing while the medicine's out, and full marks otherwise
    errands left waiting    10 off for each hour errands stood waiting at an empty store
    the council's confidence  the council's confidence in the captain: see LOYALTY AND MUTINY

Any other word is a problem the checker names.


SKILL
-----

The line each point of skill at the top says how much each point of a worker's skill either side of 5 speeds the
work or slows it, never to more than half as fast again nor less than half as fast. At 8%, Marrow's engineering 8
takes a two-hour repair down to about an hour and forty minutes, and a 2 works about a quarter slower than a 5. It
counts for the engineers' repairs (the best engineer's engineering) and for a machine whose worker is someone doing
a job (the best of them at the job's first skill: survival, for a cook). Someone with a file in world/characters has
its numbers, the player has their own, and someone without a file is a 5 in the skills of the job this file starts
them in and a 2 in everything else. Leave the line out, and skill doesn't change the pace of anything.


RISING AND FALLING
------------------

Every review day (every 7 days, from the start of the game), the officers review their people. Whoever chooses for
a post does it: the captain for the heads of the ship's departments, the first officer for the bridge officers. The
captain's own post and the speaker's aren't reviewed this way.

- A holder with a bad stretch (as many of their days on record under the mark as the line a bad stretch says) is
  warned at the midnight it shows, and relieved at the next review if it's gone on. The best of those the post is
  picked from takes it. The one relieved takes the job the new holder left, or a job that's short of hands, or goes
  to the spare hands; and a post the new holder leaves is filled in its turn.
- If whoever took over has the same trouble, whoever chooses decides it isn't the post's doing, says so once, and
  nobody's relieved over it until it's over (a day marked at the bad stretch's mark or better).
- Someone the post is picked from with two points more skill for it than whoever holds it, and an ambition of 4 or
  5, asks for it. They get it if whoever chooses thinks they'd do better. If they're told no and their temper is 4
  or 5, they work against whoever holds it: ten off each day's duty, until the memory fades. Someone passed over who
  wanted the post, with an ambition and a temper of 4 or more, does the same.
- Whoever chooses picks by ten for each point of the best skill for the post, three for each point of ambition, a
  tenth of their loyalty to the captain, half of how they feel about whoever chooses, and a little more for having
  asked or been passed over lately.

With the player as captain, it all comes to them as the first officer's recommendations, on the Command menu: do it,
keep whoever holds the post (or tell whoever asked no), or leave it to the first officer. What isn't answered by the
next review, the first officer acts on in the captain's name. The captain can also give any post the first officer or
the captain chooses for to anyone it's picked from, and hold the reviews so that nothing happens until they say so.
Someone the captain chose or kept on isn't reviewed for as many days as the captain's choices stand.

People remember being relieved, warned, passed over, told no, given a post, given one back, or kept on, by whom and for
whom, and it counts for or against whoever did it, fading by half every so many days (memories fade). The Command menu
shows it on their card, the watch bill says who was relieved, and it's the first thing they say when they're talked
to while it's fresh: the lines are in world/rooms.json, under talk, stock, grudge.

Ambition, temper and loyalty come from each person's file in world/characters (ambition: 4, temper: 2, loyalty: 70;
the first two go from 1 to 5, and loyalty from 0 to 100), or from the line everyone else at the top.


LOYALTY AND MUTINY
------------------

Everyone who holds a post but the captain has a loyalty to the captain, from 0 to 100: their file's loyalty to begin
with (or the line everyone else), what they remember of the captain, and what's moved it lately. Each of these moves a
little every day, within its bounds, and settles back once what moved it stops:

    the rations                 half rations take 1.5 a day, reduced 0.5, generous give 0.5 (from -20 to +10)
    going hungry                a day of empty kettles takes up to 4, by how many went hungry (down to -20)
    the mood aboard             a day with morale under 30 takes 1 (down to -10)
    risky jumps                 the captain jumping somewhere risky takes 2, dangerous 4, deadly 6 (down to -15)
    the sides the captain takes the captain's standing with a faction changing moves it by as much as the person's
                                beliefs agree with the faction's, and more if it's their own (from -20 to +20)

The council's confidence in the captain, from 0 to 100, follows the ship's morale, 25 less on half rations, 10 less on
reduced and 10 more on generous, less for empty kettles and for each supply that's run out, a fifth of the way a day.
It's the speaker's duty, if their line says so.

A mutiny brews on a day when morale and the officers' loyalty on average (the officers are whoever holds a post another
post chooses for) are both under the line mutiny brews; each such day adds a day of pressure, and any other takes one
away. The line the mutiny builds says after how many days each stage comes:

    grumbling          the officers whose loyalty is under the line, and about half of everyone else, grumble when the
                       player talks to them (the lines are in world/rooms.json, talk, stock, grudge, grumbling), and
                       people eating at the benches are "grumbling about the captain"
    orders refused     the council won't put a cut in the rations through for the captain while its confidence is under
                       the line, and a first officer whose loyalty is under it won't move anyone on the watch bill
    a delegation       the least loyal officer with a file leads it. With the player as captain, the officers come to
                       them together: command_delegation.txt in world/scenes, played when they carry on the story.
                       With someone else in the chair and the player aboard, whoever leads it asks them in:
                       command_conspiracy.txt. With the player away, the captain gives ground if the rations are under
                       standard, and otherwise it goes on.
    the mutiny         whoever leads it takes the chair, and their post is filled in turn. A player who was captain is
                       crew from then on, one of the spare hands with shifts to work, and, if they were aboard, under
                       guard in the brig first: command_brig.txt. An officer who'd had the chair goes to the brig.

The scenes end each way with a * mutiny line:

    give ground        the rations go up to standard, everyone remembers being heard out, the council's confidence
                       goes up 15, and the mutiny's over, with a week's calm
    calmed             the mutiny's over, with a week's calm, and nothing given
    arrest             whoever's leading it goes to the brig, if whoever keeps the brig is loyal (50 or more), and the
                       pressure halves; if they aren't, nobody moves, and the ship will be taken
    crushed            whoever's leading it goes to the brig, and the mutiny's over, with a week's calm
    step down          the player hands the chair to whoever's leading it, and is crew, with no brig
    refused            the delegation's sent away: the ship's taken the next day it brews
    taken              the ship's taken now, the brig included; the scene says what the days there were like
    released           the player's let out of the brig, as crew
    join               the player joins whoever's leading it, who takes the chair; or, if the officers would have
                       the player back as captain, they take the chair themselves
    stay out           nothing: it goes on without the player
    warn               the player tells the captain, who has whoever's leading it arrested if security's loyal; if not,
                       the ship's taken the next day it brews. Either way, whoever was leading it knows.

Whoever's put in the brig stays for the line the brig's days, off their post and the watch bill, and comes out one of
the spare hands. The officers would have the player back as captain when they were relieved of it once, and the
officers' feeling for them (their own loyalty, and what they remember of the player) is 50 or more on average.


NAMING A POST OR A JOB ELSEWHERE
--------------------------------

The other files can name a post instead of a person, as the and the post's name, and whoever holds it is the one;
or a job, and whoever the watch bill has doing it is:

    world/ship.json, a council decision:     "post": "quartermaster"
    world/systems/<place>.txt:               guards: the chief of security, the guards
                                             engineers: the chief engineer, the techs
    world/logistics/<place>.txt, an errand:  - the quartermaster: ration crates from the shuttle stores to ...
                                             - a tech: fuel cells from the shuttle stores to the fuel rack ...
    world/logistics/<place>.txt, a machine:  ... with power and a cook there

An errand that names a job goes to whoever's doing it, awake and not carrying something else already. A machine
that names a job needs someone doing that job in its room; one that says someone, anyone.

A council decision says {who} as the post and the holder's name (Quartermaster Brandt), and the council does it
itself if nobody holds the post, or whoever does has been lost.

The ship's own lines in world/ship.json can name whoever holds a post by its name with underscores in curly brackets,
such as {quartermaster}, {chief_engineer} or {chief_medical_officer}: that's their own title and name while they hold
the post the game began them in (Dr. Aduro), and the post's name and theirs otherwise (Quartermaster Benno).
