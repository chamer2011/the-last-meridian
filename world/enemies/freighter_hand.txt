In a line:
A deckhand off a freighter, with a cargo hook in one fist and no wish to be standing where they are.

When the fight starts:
- A deckhand plants their feet across the hatch, with a cargo hook held low.

When they hit:
- The hook comes round and catches you on the forearm.
- They shoulder you into a stack of crates, and the edge of one finds your ribs.

When they miss:
- The hook bites into a crate beside you and sticks there.
- They swing, and you're not where they thought you'd be.

When they hit hard:
- The hook comes down overhand with their whole weight behind it, and it lands.

When they're beaten:
- The deckhand sits down hard among the crates, and the hook clatters on the deck.
- They go down, and stay down, one hand up to say they're done.

When they join the fight:
- {Enemy} comes down the hold ladder two rungs at a time, hook in hand.

When they're stunned:
- {Enemy} sways where they stand, the hook hanging loose.

When persuading works:
- You tell them the cargo isn't theirs to bleed for. They look at the hatch, and at you, and they step aside.

When persuading fails:
- You tell them nobody's paying them enough for this. They say they know what they're paid.

When paying works:
- They weigh the credits, and then the hook, and the hook goes back on its ring.

When a threat works:
- You tell them how this ends. They back up against the crates with both hands open.
