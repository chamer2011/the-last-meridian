HOW TO WRITE A PORT
===================

Every file in this folder is a port: what it holds, what comes out of the ground there, what it makes and from
what, and what it uses, in real amounts. A file's name is the place's id, as in world/places and on the star
map: frontier.txt is Tessen Colony. Files that start with an underscore, like this one, aren't read. The goods
are named as world/goods names them.

    # Tessen Colony
    draft: the AI's, not yet edited by the owner
    people: 2,000

    Holds:
    - grain: usually 3,300
    - meal: usually 3,300
    - ration crates: usually 5,800
    - fuel cells: usually 150, from Cinder Reach

    Ground:
    - the Long Fields: 330 grain a day

    Makes:
    - the drying sheds: 330 meal a day, from 330 grain
    - the homesteads: 580 ration crates a day, from 290 meal

    Uses:
    - the colonists: 20 ration crates, 10 drinking water a day
    - its power: 6 fuel cells a day

WHAT HAPPENS EACH MIDNIGHT

1. What's on its way to the port comes a day nearer, and what arrives is put away.
2. Its ground gives what its Ground lines say, to the hands there are, and its makers make what their lines
   say. Neither goes past what the port usually holds: a full store stops them. Each maker takes what its line
   says it makes things from, out of what the port holds, and makes no more than there is of it. A port that's
   hungry, thirsty, sick or out of power makes less (see MAKES).
3. What its Uses lines say is used up.
4. For each good it sends elsewhere for, when a haul would leave it with no more than a little over what it
   usually holds, it sends for one: to the ports its Holds line names, the best stocked first, sharing what it
   needs between them. What's sent leaves the sender that day and arrives days later: a day to load and unload,
   and a day for every fifteen squares on the star map, two days at the least and six at the most.

A sender sends whole hauls while it has four fifths of what it usually holds or more, less below that, and
nothing once it's down to three tenths, which it keeps for itself. So a port whose makers have stopped runs
down, then sends less, and the ports it supplies run short some days after it, and recover when the hauls
come again. One haul of a good is on its way from each sender at a time, and a second once the port is badly
short.

A shortage travels up a tree the same way, from the ground. A field that fails gives no grain; the sheds mill
no meal; the homesteads pack no crates; and the ports that send there for food run short. And it crosses the
map before anything is finished: a port that makes medicine from another port's extracts runs out of extracts
some days after that port stops sending them. The star map says so under the supply: "Its makers are short of
extracts (nothing's leaving Lumen Sanctuary)."

A port's word for a supply (plenty, normal, short, scarce, almost gone) is how many it has against what it
usually holds: with all of it, it's normal; with four fifths or less, short; with half or less, scarce. What
things cost there follows the word, as it always did.

THE TOP

The first line is the port's name, after a #.

draft:    who wrote it. The game doesn't read it.
people:   how many live there. The game doesn't use the number: it's for whoever writes the lines, since what a
          port uses goes by its people. The drafts took, for a thousand people in a day: 10 ration crates, 5
          drinking water, 3 fuel cells, 1 jump fuel, 1 beer and spirits, 0.5 ration packs, 0.5 air bottles,
          0.3 parts crates, 0.3 tea and tobacco, 0.2 medicine crates, 0.2 clothes, and less of the rest.

HOLDS

    - ration crates: usually 4,500, from Tessen Colony or Maris Deep

The good, a colon, usually and how many it has when things are as usual, then from and the ports it sends to for
more, with or between them. A port is named by its name, the name the star map gives it, or its id, and has to
have a file here that holds the good. Leave the from out for a good the port makes itself or has ground for,
or one that only ever goes down.

A port holds everything that comes out of its ground, everything its makers make, and everything they make
things from: Haven holds meal because its kitchens pack ration packs from it, though nobody there eats meal.

usually is what the word for a supply is measured against, and where the port's ground and its makers stop.
The drafts gave a port that sends for a good twenty-five days' worth of what goes through it, and a port that
makes one twenty days' worth of what's taken from it (and never less than six of the biggest haul that leaves
it): enough that a single haul never makes it short.

GROUND

    - the Long Fields: 330 grain a day
    - the Ice Cut: 190 ice a day
    - the blood chits on the Gallery: 16 blood a day

What comes out of the ground here: where, a colon, how many of what at the most, and a day. Only a basic good
does (the Basic step of a tree in world/goods), and the port has to hold it. A Ground line has no from: this
is the one place in the game where something comes from nothing. Ground is whatever a good comes from first:
a field, a well, a seam, a ring of ice, a field of wrecks, the sea, a cloud, and on Anselm's Mercy the people
who pay in blood.

The ground gives to whatever hands there are: a hungry, thirsty or sick port brings in less, as its makers
make less. It needs no power. A port with no ground for something it works up sends to another port for it,
like Lumen for the wool and the kelp thread its looms weave, or goes without: a station has no ground at all,
and neither has a ship any water of its own.

MAKES

    - the kitchens on the Manifest Docks: 72 ration packs a day, from 36 meal
    - the charging sheds: 430 fuel cells a day, from 43 refined fuel, 8.6 parts crates

Who makes it, a colon, how many of what, and a day; then from and what goes into that many, with commas
between. So the kitchens make 72 cases of ration packs on a day they work flat out, and take 36 sacks of meal
for them: a sack to two cases. On a day they make less, they take that much less. Change how many a maker
makes and change what goes into it as well, or each one will take more or less than it did.

The port has to hold the good and everything that goes into it, and what goes into it has to be something
world/goods says the good is made from (its "made from"). Every maker has a from: only the ground gives
something for nothing, and a basic good is never written here.

How much a maker makes on a day is the least of:
- what its line says, when nothing's in its way;
- the room there is: it stops at what the port usually holds;
- what there is of each thing it makes it from;
- its hands and its power. A port that's short of what its people eat or drink has fewer hands at its makers,
  down to three quarters of their work; one that's scarce of medicine has more of them laid up, down to 85
  hundredths; one that's scarce of what its makers run on has less power, down to four tenths of their work.
  Which goods those are is written at the top of the trees in world/goods (hands need, the sick need, power
  is), and the makers of the tree the power comes from don't need it.

The makers work in the order the goods are made in, whatever order they're written in: what's out of the
ground first, then what's refined, then what's made of it, so that today's grain goes into today's meal and
today's meal into today's crates. Two makers that take the same good are served in the order they're written.

The drafts gave each maker and each piece of ground twice what's taken from it in a day, so that it catches
up after a bad stretch, and enough to keep whatever is made from its goods here going flat out. One that
gives less than is taken from it is something npm run check-story says.

USES

    - its people: 60 ration crates, 3 ration packs, 30 drinking water a day

Who uses it, a colon, how many of each thing with commas between them, and a day. The port has to hold each of
them. As many lines as you like: its people, ships calling, its power, its sick, its docks. Who it is only
matters to whoever reads it. What its makers take isn't written here: their own lines say it.

WHAT GOES ON THERE

world/daily.json's events are the things that happen at a port (a strike, a fever, a harvest), and each says
what it does to a supply while it lasts: its makers make less or more, its people use more, goods are taken off
its market or brought to it, nothing gets in or out, or what was sent there never arrives. Makers and what gets
in or out go for every good of that supply's tree, the ground's and the refined ones too: a harvest is twice
the grain out of the fields, and a storm a tenth of the kelp. What's taken off the market or brought to it is
only the finished goods, the developed and the advanced. That file's own first lines explain the words.

WHAT'S NOT HERE YET

The ship herself still buys by the lot, and what she buys or sells is taken from the port or added to it in
real crates. She still fills her shuttle stores from nothing: living off the ports is the next step. Freight,
a port's goods in its rooms, and the ships that carry the hauls come with later steps: until then a haul is an
amount on its way, which the star map says when a port is short.

CHECKING AND CHANGING

npm run check-story reads every file here and says, in plain words, what it can't follow: a good that isn't in
world/goods, a port that isn't on the star map, a good that comes from a port that holds none, a maker of
something the port doesn't hold, a maker that takes something the port doesn't hold or that the good isn't
made from, a maker with no from, a basic good written under Makes or a made one under Ground, a good that's
used or taken and never comes, or one that's taken faster than it's made or comes out of the ground.

The Story board has Ports: each line of a port's file is a box, with the ports it sends to on the left and the
ports that send to it on the right, what comes out of the ground above its makers, an arrow from each of them
to what it gives or makes, and one to each maker from each thing it's made from. Click a box to change its
line, then Keep and Save.
