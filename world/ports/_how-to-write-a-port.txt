HOW TO WRITE A PORT
===================

Every file in this folder is a port: what it holds, what it makes and from what, and what it uses, in real
amounts. A file's name is the place's id, as in world/places and on the star map: haven.txt. Files that start
with an underscore, like this one, aren't read. The goods are named as world/goods names them.

    # Haven Station
    draft: the AI's, not yet edited by the owner
    people: 6,000

    Holds:
    - meal: usually 450, from Tessen Colony
    - ration crates: usually 4,500, from Tessen Colony or Maris Deep
    - ration packs: usually 720
    - water: usually 150, from Tessen Colony or Maris Deep
    - drinking water: usually 600

    Makes:
    - the kitchens on the Manifest Docks: 72 ration packs a day, from 36 meal
    - the casking line: 60 drinking water a day, from 12 water

    Uses:
    - its people: 60 ration crates, 3 ration packs, 30 drinking water a day
    - the forty berths: 12 jump fuel a day

WHAT HAPPENS EACH MIDNIGHT

1. What's on its way to the port comes a day nearer, and what arrives is put away.
2. Its makers make what their lines say, but never past what the port usually holds: a full store stops them.
   Each takes what its line says it makes things from, out of what the port holds, and makes no more than
   there is of it. A port that's hungry, thirsty, sick or out of power makes less (see MAKES).
3. What its Uses lines say is used up.
4. For each good it sends elsewhere for, when a haul would leave it with no more than a little over what it
   usually holds, it sends for one: to the ports its Holds line names, the best stocked first, sharing what it
   needs between them. What's sent leaves the sender that day and arrives days later: a day to load and unload,
   and a day for every fifteen squares on the star map, two days at the least and six at the most.

A sender sends whole hauls while it has four fifths of what it usually holds or more, less below that, and
nothing once it's down to three tenths, which it keeps for itself. So a port whose makers have stopped runs
down, then sends less, and the ports it supplies run short some days after it, and recover when the hauls
come again. One haul of a good is on its way from each sender at a time, and a second once the port is badly
short.

A shortage travels down a tree the same way. A port that makes medicine from another port's extracts runs out
of extracts some days after that port stops sending them, then makes no medicine, and then the ports it sends
medicine to run short. The star map says so under the supply: "Its makers are short of extracts (nothing's
leaving Lumen Sanctuary)."

A port's word for a supply (plenty, normal, short, scarce, almost gone) is how many it has against what it
usually holds: with all of it, it's normal; with four fifths or less, short; with half or less, scarce. What
things cost there follows the word, as it always did.

THE TOP

The first line is the port's name, after a #.

draft:    who wrote it. The game doesn't read it.
people:   how many live there. The game doesn't use the number: it's for whoever writes the lines, since what a
          port uses goes by its people. The drafts took, for a thousand people in a day: 10 ration crates, 5
          drinking water, 3 fuel cells, 1 jump fuel, 1 beer and spirits, 0.5 ration packs, 0.5 air bottles,
          0.3 parts crates, 0.3 tea and tobacco, 0.2 medicine crates, 0.2 clothes, and less of the rest.

HOLDS

    - ration crates: usually 4,500, from Tessen Colony or Maris Deep

The good, a colon, usually and how many it has when things are as usual, then from and the ports it sends to for
more, with or between them. A port is named by its name, the name the star map gives it, or its id, and has to
have a file here that holds the good. Leave the from out for a good the port makes itself, or one that only ever
goes down.

A port holds everything its makers make, and everything they make things from: Haven holds meal because its
kitchens pack ration packs from it, though nobody there eats meal.

usually is what the word for a supply is measured against, and where the port's makers stop. The drafts gave a
port that sends for a good twenty-five days' worth of what goes through it, and a port that makes one twenty
days' worth of what's taken from it (and never less than six of the biggest haul that leaves it): enough that
a single haul never makes it short.

MAKES

    - the kitchens on the Manifest Docks: 72 ration packs a day, from 36 meal
    - the charging sheds: 430 fuel cells a day, from 43 refined fuel, 8.6 parts crates
    - the drying sheds: 310 meal a day

Who makes it, a colon, how many of what, and a day; then from and what goes into that many, with commas
between. So the kitchens make 72 cases of ration packs on a day they work flat out, and take 36 sacks of meal
for them: a sack to two cases. On a day they make less, they take that much less. Change how many a maker
makes and change what goes into it as well, or each one will take more or less than it did.

The port has to hold the good and everything that goes into it, and what goes into it has to be something
world/goods says the good is made from (its "made from"). A maker of a good that's made from nothing there
(the refined goods, for now) has no from.

How much a maker makes on a day is the least of:
- what its line says, when nothing's in its way;
- the room there is: it stops at what the port usually holds;
- what there is of each thing it makes it from;
- its hands and its power. A port that's short of what its people eat or drink has fewer hands at its makers,
  down to three quarters of their work; one that's scarce of medicine has more of them laid up, down to 85
  hundredths; one that's scarce of what its makers run on has less power, down to four tenths of their work.
  Which goods those are is written at the top of the trees in world/goods (hands need, the sick need, power
  is), and the makers of the tree the power comes from don't need it.

The makers work in the order the goods are made in, whatever order they're written in: what's refined first,
then what's made of it, so that today's meal goes into today's crates. Two makers that take the same good are
served in the order they're written.

The drafts gave each maker twice what's taken from it in a day, so that it catches up after a bad stretch, and
a maker of something that's made into other things here enough to keep those going flat out. A maker that
makes less than is taken from it is something npm run check-story says.

USES

    - its people: 60 ration crates, 3 ration packs, 30 drinking water a day

Who uses it, a colon, how many of each thing with commas between them, and a day. The port has to hold each of
them. As many lines as you like: its people, ships calling, its power, its sick, its docks. Who it is only
matters to whoever reads it. What its makers take isn't written here: their own lines say it.

WHAT GOES ON THERE

world/daily.json's events are the things that happen at a port (a strike, a fever, a harvest), and each says
what it does to a supply while it lasts: its makers make less or more, its people use more, goods are taken off
its market or brought to it, nothing gets in or out, or what was sent there never arrives. Makers and what gets
in or out go for every good of that supply's tree, the refined ones too; what's taken off the market or
brought to it is only the finished goods, the developed and the advanced. That file's own first lines explain
the words.

WHAT'S NOT HERE YET

The basic goods, and the ground they come out of, are the last part of this step: until then the refined goods
are made from nothing. The ship herself still buys by the lot, and what she buys or sells is taken from the
port or added to it in real crates. Freight, a port's goods in its rooms, and the ships that carry the hauls
come with later steps: until then a haul is an amount on its way, which the star map says when a port is short.

CHECKING AND CHANGING

npm run check-story reads every file here and says, in plain words, what it can't follow: a good that isn't in
world/goods, a port that isn't on the star map, a good that comes from a port that holds none, a maker of
something the port doesn't hold, a maker that takes something the port doesn't hold or that the good isn't
made from, a maker that says nothing of what it makes a good from, a good that's used or taken and never
comes, or one that's taken faster than it's made.

The Story board has Ports: each line of a port's file is a box, with the ports it sends to on the left and the
ports that send to it on the right, an arrow from each maker to what it makes, and one to it from each thing
it's made from. Click a box to change its line, then Keep and Save.
